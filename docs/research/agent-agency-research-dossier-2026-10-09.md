# ScoutSA Research Dossier 01 — Football Agents and Agencies
**Research date:** 2026-10-09  
**Status:** Desk research; not direct Vaal Triangle validation  
**Decision supported:** Define what ScoutSA must understand about agents/agencies and avoid confusing talent discovery, representation, trials and recruitment.  
**Claim discipline:** Findings below are limited to the cited sources. FIFA/SAFA regulations are not a legal opinion, and the SAFA document found is approved 9 December 2023; verify whether amendments, injunctions or later directives affect application before operationalizing any rule.

## 1. Executive findings

1. **An agent, agency, scout, academy and club are not interchangeable.** FIFA defines a football agent as a natural person licensed by FIFA to perform football-agent services for a client with the purpose of concluding a transaction. FIFA's definition of agent services includes transaction-related negotiation and communication. A scout's observation of talent, an academy's training service, and a club's recruitment decision are different activities. [FIFA FAQ](https://inside.fifa.com/transfer-system/agents/faq-agents)
2. **Representation should be evidenced, not inferred from social-media status or a company logo.** FIFA says agent services require a written representation agreement, with parties, duration, fee, services and signatures. FIFA's official FAQ says a player/coach agreement may not exceed two years under the framework it describes, and requires written notice about independent legal advice. [FIFA FAQ](https://inside.fifa.com/transfer-system/agents/faq-agents)
3. **Minors require a particularly strict approach.** FIFA's parent education material says agent licensing alone is not sufficient for representing minors: additional authorization is required. It describes prior written legal-guardian consent and restrictions around when a minor may be approached. SAFA's published regulations also contain a minor-representation section. Exact application must be checked against current rules and local law. [FIFA parents' guide](https://inside.fifa.com/transfer-system/agents/education/fifa-parent-education-on-football-agents/preparation-to-engage) · [SAFA regulations PDF](https://www.safa.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/SAFA-FOOTBALL-AGENT-REGULATIONS-Approved-by-NEC-on-09-December-2023_compressed.pdf)
4. **Fake-agent risk is not theoretical.** FIFPRO Africa published survey findings about approaches by people purporting to be agents: social-media and in-person approaches, promises of trials/contracts, and many reported bad or incomplete experiences. These are findings from the cited campaign/survey and should not be treated as a prevalence estimate for the Vaal Triangle. [FIFPRO Africa campaign](https://www.fifpro.org/en/articles/2023/07/fifpro-africa-launches-campaign-warning-footballers-about-fake-agents)
5. **A promised trial is not proof of a real opportunity.** FIFPRO recommends verifying the club and intermediary, and resolving practical questions about travel, accommodation, costs, visas/work permission, injury cover and what happens if the trial fails. FIFA's 2026 parent education resources also cover fake agents, representation agreements, inducements, trial checklists and international trials. [FIFPRO Africa](https://www.fifpro.org/en/articles/2023/07/fifpro-africa-launches-campaign-warning-footballers-about-fake-agents) · [FIFA parent education hub](https://inside.fifa.com/transfer-system/agents/education/fifa-parent-education-on-football-agents)
6. **The financial relationship creates possible conflicts that should be disclosed.** FIFA's FAQ describes the general client-pays principle and restrictions on representing multiple parties in the same transaction, with a defined exception for permitted dual representation subject to advance written agreement. SAFA's published 2023 regulations contain domestic service-fee and dual-representation provisions. Do not present one fee rule as universal across all contexts. [FIFA FAQ](https://inside.fifa.com/transfer-system/agents/faq-agents) · [SAFA regulations PDF](https://www.safa.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/SAFA-FOOTBALL-AGENT-REGULATIONS-Approved-by-NEC-on-09-December-2023_compressed.pdf)
7. **ScoutSA should not become an unlicensed intermediary or promise placements.** The safer MVP role is to help establish provenance, distinguish roles, display genuine opportunity details, support purpose-limited discovery/contact and record the status of a request. Any activity that itself constitutes regulated agent services needs separate legal analysis.

## 2. Primary-source lane

### 2.1 FIFA agent FAQ

Publisher: FIFA, official agent-regulations FAQ.  
URL: https://inside.fifa.com/transfer-system/agents/faq-agents

Key points in the opened source:
- A football agent is a natural person licensed by FIFA.
- Agent services are linked to a transaction, including relevant negotiation and communication.
- A written representation agreement is required before the agent performs agent services for the client.
- Minimum agreement terms include parties, duration, service fee, nature of services and signatures.
- FIFA describes the general client-pays model and service-fee caps, with defined exceptions.
- Conflict-of-interest rules generally restrict acting for multiple parties in a single transaction, subject to a limited permitted dual-representation route with prior written agreement.
- National member associations enforce domestic matters; FIFA's jurisdiction depends on international dimensions and other circumstances.

Caution: football-agent regulation has had legal and implementation developments. Do not copy fee caps or jurisdictional assumptions into product logic without a current jurisdiction-specific legal check.

### 2.2 FIFA Parents' Education on Football Agents

Publisher: FIFA; the online education hub and handbook were published in March 2026.  
URLs:
- https://inside.fifa.com/transfer-system/agents/education/fifa-parent-education-on-football-agents
- https://inside.fifa.com/transfer-system/agents/education/fifa-parent-education-on-football-agents/preparation-to-engage
- https://inside.fifa.com/transfer-system/agents/education/fifa-parent-education-on-football-agents/entering-into-a-representation-relationship

The material covers choosing and verifying agents, representation agreements, inducements, working with agents, ending relationships, fake agents, trial arrangements and international-trial considerations. FIFA states that a general agent licence alone does not automatically authorize work with minors; additional authorization is required. The source also highlights parental support, child participation in decisions and safeguarding.

Product implication: a “verified agent” label should not imply that every transaction, opportunity, claim or proposed interaction is safe. Verification should be scoped to a specific identity, licence/status, time and purpose; the system must not infer that an agent is authorized to represent a particular minor merely because a licence exists.

### 2.3 SAFA Football Agent Regulations

Publisher: South African Football Association.  
Document title: *SAFA Football Agent Regulations — Approved by NEC on 09 December 2023*.  
URL: https://www.safa.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/SAFA-FOOTBALL-AGENT-REGULATIONS-Approved-by-NEC-on-09-December-2023_compressed.pdf

The published document defines a football agent as a natural person licensed by FIFA. It includes sections on representation agreements, minors, service fees, rights/obligations, disclosure, disputes and disciplinary matters. Its minor-representation section states that an approach to a minor or legal guardian concerning football-agent services may only be made within the specified period before the minor reaches the applicable age for a first professional contract, and only after prior written consent is obtained. The document's fee section sets out South African rand-denominated thresholds and percentages.

Caution: this document's approval date is December 2023. The research establishes that SAFA published it, not that every clause remains enforceable and unchanged as of this research date. Obtain current legal/regulatory confirmation before using it as operational policy.

### 2.4 FIFA's official parent education news

Publisher: FIFA, 12 March 2026.  
URL: https://inside.fifa.com/legal/news/fifa-launches-parental-handbook-to-support-families-navigating-agent

FIFA says the handbook was developed with the Football Agent Working Group and Loughborough University. It responds to concerns about minors being targeted by people falsely presenting themselves as agents, including unfounded promises, payment requests and charges for services not delivered. This is official explanation of why FIFA created the education resource, not an independent prevalence study.

## 3. Independent / player-protection lane

### 3.1 FIFPRO Africa — fake-agent campaign (2023)

Publisher: FIFPRO, 2023.  
URL: https://www.fifpro.org/en/articles/2023/07/fifpro-africa-launches-campaign-warning-footballers-about-fake-agents

The article reports survey findings that players were approached by people purporting to be agents, sometimes via social media and sometimes in person. It reports that many players said promised trials or contracts did not materialize, and that many had not been educated about agents. It recommends checking agent registration/licensing, speaking directly with the agent, seeking legal advice before signing, checking that the club knows about the trial, and clarifying trial logistics and costs.

Limitations: this is a player-union campaign and its cited survey; the available article does not establish that its sample represents all African players or South African grassroots football. Use it as credible risk evidence, not as a Vaal Triangle rate.

### 3.2 FIFPRO — “Eight ways to spot a fake agent” (2020)

Publisher: FIFPRO, 24 July 2020.  
URL: https://www.fifpro.org/en/articles/2020/07/eight-ways-to-spot-a-fake-agent

The article warns about demands for registration, travel, accommodation or other money before a trial or contract. It is an educational warning article, not proof that every upfront charge in every football-related service is fraudulent. For ScoutSA, the actionable lesson is to require transparent cost responsibility and independently confirm the opportunity with the receiving club, rather than relying on a claimant's assurance.

### 3.3 Professional Footballers' Association — fake intermediaries (2021)

Publisher: The PFA, 11 February 2021.  
URL: https://www.thepfa.com/news/2021/2/11/fake-intermediaries-remain-vigilant

The PFA described cases in which people claiming links to clubs approached players about overseas trials/contracts and asked for advance fees. Its practical advice was to check registration and contact the club directly to confirm that the intermediary and trial are known to it. This is a UK player-union warning, so it informs the control design but is not a South African rule.

### 3.4 South African media example: purported Manchester United soccer-school offer (2023)

Publisher: TimesLIVE / Consumer Live, 3 August 2023.  
URL: https://www.timeslive.co.za/news/consumer-live/2023-08-03-offer-to-join-manchester-united-soccer-school-is-a-scam/

The report describes a purported Manchester United Soccer School offer that SAFA's representative said was a scam. The example shows why a prestigious club name or a document handed through a school is not enough to establish authenticity. Confirm the specific program through independently sourced official club/association channels.

### 3.5 South African allegation reported in 2026 — use cautiously

URL: https://wmsportsnews.wordpress.com/2026/07/15/wm-sports-news-investigation-young-player-allegedly-scammed-r2000-after-being-promised-magesi-fc-assessment-by-man-claiming-to-represent-the-club/

This report describes a young player's allegation that R2,000 was paid for a promised Magesi FC assessment. The article says its follow-up found the named individual was no longer on the club's official database as an employee, agent or representative. This is a single outlet's report about an allegation and its own checks; it is not a court finding. It is not sufficient to conclude guilt or estimate prevalence. It does, however, illustrate the need for direct club confirmation and a clear record of who is authorized to offer an assessment.

## 4. Media / video research path

The most defensible starting video/media collection is the official FIFA Parents' Education hub, which links to the handbook and multiple practical guides:
https://inside.fifa.com/transfer-system/agents/education/fifa-parent-education-on-football-agents

The hub includes material on:
- agent verification;
- questions to ask when choosing an agent;
- fake agents;
- representation agreements;
- inducements;
- international trials;
- checklists for a child's trial;
- fees;
- trafficking risks.

A video should be treated as first-person evidence only for what the speaker personally witnessed or claims to have experienced. Agency promotional videos establish how the agency presents itself, not independent proof of its success rate, licence, relationships or trial availability. For any agency video selected later, record speaker, date, direct URL, whether promotional/interview/documentary, and the exact claim being evaluated. No unverified YouTube search result is used here as substantive evidence.

## 5. What ScoutSA can responsibly infer

### Supported enough to design around
- Role distinction matters: agent, agency, scout, academy and club are different entities/claims.
- Agent identity/licensing and a specific opportunity should be independently checked.
- A representation relationship has contractual terms; a public profile is not a substitute.
- Youth-agent interaction requires heightened controls and applicable authorization/consent checks.
- Trial details must include the receiving club, date/location, costs, travel/accommodation, supervision, insurance/injury arrangements and fallback plan.
- Financial interests and representation conflicts can affect incentives and should not be hidden.
- A player should not be led to believe a trial, contract or placement is guaranteed.

### Not established by this research
- The prevalence of fake agents in the Vaal Triangle.
- Whether local players' primary bottleneck is transport, cost, guardian availability, trust, discovery, coaching access, documentation or something else.
- Whether local agencies or clubs would adopt ScoutSA.
- That every request for money is fraudulent in every context.
- That a listed person or organization is currently licensed; this must be checked against current authoritative records.
- That ScoutSA would be legally compliant by implementing these ideas.
- That publishing a player's profile necessarily improves opportunity or results in placement.

## 6. Product implications to carry forward

1. **Separate entity from role:** organization profile, natural-person identity, role/affiliation and current authorization should not be collapsed into one “verified” status.
2. **Verify claims at their source:** independently confirm a trial with the receiving club or organization using contact details found independently, not only those supplied by the person advertising it.
3. **Scope every verification:** identify what was checked, by whom, when, against which source, and what it does not prove.
4. **Record opportunity terms:** exact club, date, location, responsible contact, eligibility, cost responsibilities, travel/accommodation, guardian arrangements, supervision and cancellation/failure contingencies.
5. **Do not sell access to hope:** avoid placement guarantees and avoid presenting payment to ScoutSA as a route to being selected.
6. **Youth protection is a workflow, not a badge:** use applicable guardian/child participation, consent, safeguarding and disclosure controls; do not assume agent licence equals safeguarding clearance.
7. **Disclose conflicts and financial relationships:** don't represent a paid recommendation as neutral.
8. **Do not become an agent by accident:** obtain a South African legal review of the boundary between information/referral services and regulated football-agent services before building or operating functionality that negotiates, represents or arranges transactions.

## 7. Questions for the next research phases

### Coaches and scouts
- What evidence do coaches trust when they do not know a player personally?
- What causes them to ignore a profile or trial request?
- How are opportunities communicated today, and who is allowed to submit a player?
- Do they need verified data, video, match context, references or live observation most?
- How do they handle players who are promising but cannot access a trial?

### Individual players
- How do players hear about opportunities?
- Which promises have they received, and how did they verify them?
- What costs arise before, during and after a trial?
- What happens when the player cannot travel or a trial fails?
- What information would they be willing to share, with whom, and for what purpose?
- Which existing pathways work well enough that ScoutSA should not duplicate them?

### Legal / safeguarding
- What current FIFA/SAFA rules apply to a South African grassroots discovery/referral platform?
- At what point does the platform's conduct constitute regulated agent services?
- What current rules govern approaching/representing minors, guardian consent and fees?
- What youth-safeguarding, privacy, data-sharing and retention obligations apply?

## Research conclusion

The best-supported immediate design direction is not “build an agent marketplace.” It is: **help players and families distinguish real people, real roles and real opportunities; verify the opportunity with the receiving organization; make costs and responsibilities explicit; and avoid guaranteeing outcomes.**

This conclusion is a research-derived design recommendation, not direct stakeholder validation, a legal opinion or proof of local demand.
