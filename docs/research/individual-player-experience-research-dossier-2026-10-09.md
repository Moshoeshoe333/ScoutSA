# ScoutSA Research Dossier 03 — Individual Players' Experiences and Access Barriers
**Research date:** 2026-10-09  
**Status:** Desk research; no direct Vaal Triangle interviews obtained for this dossier  
**Decision supported:** Identify player-side risks and practical constraints without assuming every player's experience is the same.

## 1. Executive findings

1. **A 2026 South African township-player vox pop reported practical needs beyond scouting exposure.** African Reporter / Caxton reported comments from local players in Tsakani about transport assistance, equipment, boots, coaching, community support and access to scouts/development academies. This is a small, journalistic vox pop with named quotations, not a representative survey. It is geographically more relevant than international examples, but it is not evidence specific to the Vaal Triangle. [African Reporter, 13 February 2026](https://www.citizen.co.za/african-reporter/news-headlines/2026/02/13/township-soccer-players-speak-out-on-their-biggest-needs/)
2. **African professional players have reported serious risks from false trial/contract promises.** FIFPRO Africa's 2023 campaign summarized a survey of 263 professional players from seven countries: more than 70% said someone contacted them claiming to help them move clubs; 56% of those approached did not receive a promised trial, and 44% did not sign the contract they expected. These figures concern the survey sample, not all African players or South African grassroots players. [FIFPRO Africa](https://www.fifpro.org/en/articles/2023/07/fifpro-africa-launches-campaign-warning-footballers-about-fake-agents)
3. **A player's risk can extend beyond losing money.** FIFPRO documented a case involving Ghanaian players who travelled to Zimbabwe after being promised a club opportunity, then found the promised club did not exist and became stranded. FIFPRO reported that player unions helped arrange their return. This is a case report, not a prevalence estimate. [FIFPRO case report](https://www.fifpro.org/en/supporting-players/conditions-of-employment/the-transfer-of-players/players-had-to-live-like-beggars-after-being-scammed-by-fake-agents)
4. **A separate cross-border case illustrates how a referral chain can create misplaced trust.** FIFPRO reported that Kenyan player Davis Agesa Amuzu was approached by a local coach who introduced him to a person claiming to be an agent for a Malaysian club; he paid travel-related costs and an agent fee, arrived, and could not reach the promised contacts. The report says player associations helped him return. This is an individual account, not evidence that coaches generally are untrustworthy. [FIFPRO, 11 October 2023](https://www.fifpro.org/en/supporting-players/safe-working-environments/fake-agents/malaysian-and-kenyan-unions-help-stranded-player-return-home-after-being-cheated-by-fake-agent/)
5. **Player-protection guidance focuses on independently verifying the destination and terms.** FIFPRO advises players to establish the exact club and country, whether the club and manager know about the trial, who pays travel/accommodation, what happens if the trial fails, and what happens if the player is injured. [FIFPRO Africa](https://www.fifpro.org/en/articles/2023/07/fifpro-africa-launches-campaign-warning-footballers-about-fake-agents)
6. **Players' needs are broader than discovery.** The reviewed accounts identify potential needs involving equipment, training resources, transport, exposure, credible information, costs, travel, safe accommodation and contingency planning. The evidence does not establish which of these is the dominant barrier for Vaal Triangle players.

## 2. Local / South African player voice lane

### African Reporter / Caxton — Tsakani township players (13 February 2026)
URL: https://www.citizen.co.za/african-reporter/news-headlines/2026/02/13/township-soccer-players-speak-out-on-their-biggest-needs/

The article reports:
- Siphamandla Zulu called for more training equipment such as balls and bibs.
- Blessing Selala emphasized transport assistance because players can miss games or training when travel costs cannot be met.
- Luzuko Ntombeni called for exposure to scouts and development academies, saying talented township players can be overlooked.
- It also records calls for community support, suitable boots and access to professional coaching.

Interpretation:
- Practical attendance, development resources and discovery can coexist as needs.
- ScoutSA should not assume that publishing a player profile solves transport or training-resource gaps.
- If opportunity access is included in the product, it should record constraints without labelling the player as less talented.

Limitations:
- A small vox pop; no sampling frame, denominator or method for generalizing prevalence.
- Tsakani is not the Vaal Triangle. Treat it as a South African contextual signal and a question to investigate, not a local finding.
- The article page was surfaced by search but could not be independently opened in this research pass; retain reduced source confidence until rechecked.

## 3. Player protection / cross-border evidence lane

### 3.1 FIFPRO Africa — survey and practical trial checklist (5 July 2023)
URL: https://www.fifpro.org/en/articles/2023/07/fifpro-africa-launches-campaign-warning-footballers-about-fake-agents

FIFPRO states that 263 professional players from Botswana, Cameroon, DR Congo, Ghana, Kenya, Zambia and Zimbabwe took part in its survey. The article reports that:
- more than 70% of the sample were contacted by someone claiming to help them move clubs;
- 56% of players approached did not get the trial promised;
- 44% did not sign the contract they expected;
- 70% said they had not been educated about agents/intermediaries.

FIFPRO advises players to verify agent licensing, speak directly with the person, seek legal advice before signing, check with the receiving club, and clarify travel, accommodation, cost, visa/work permission, injury cover and what happens if the trial is unsuccessful.

Limitations:
- Survey participants were professional players from seven countries, not a representative sample of South African grassroots players.
- The campaign has a player-protection purpose. That is appropriate for its topic, but statistics should be quoted with sample context.

### 3.2 FIFPRO — Ghanaian players stranded in Zimbabwe (6 October 2020)
URL: https://www.fifpro.org/en/supporting-players/conditions-of-employment/the-transfer-of-players/players-had-to-live-like-beggars-after-being-scammed-by-fake-agents

FIFPRO reports that four Ghanaian footballers were promised a club opportunity in Zimbabwe; on arrival, the promised club did not exist as represented, and players were left without reliable accommodation or money. The article says player unions and other parties helped arrange return travel and reports that the relevant officials were sanctioned by the Zimbabwe association.

This account demonstrates a possible failure chain: trusted referral → unverifiable club/offer → travel expenditure → no genuine opportunity → unsafe or unsupported situation. It is a case report and does not establish how common such cases are.

### 3.3 FIFPRO — Kenyan player stranded in Malaysia (11 October 2023)
URL: https://www.fifpro.org/en/supporting-players/safe-working-environments/fake-agents/malaysian-and-kenyan-unions-help-stranded-player-return-home-after-being-cheated-by-fake-agent/

FIFPRO reports that Davis Agesa Amuzu was introduced by a local coach to someone claiming to be an agent for a Malaysian club. He paid for travel-related items and a fee, arrived in Malaysia, and could not contact the people who were supposed to meet him. The report says the Kenyan and Malaysian player associations, supported by FIFPRO, arranged his return.

Interpretation:
- A trusted introduction is not independent confirmation of the final club or opportunity.
- Verify through independently sourced official club contacts before travel, payment or sharing sensitive documents.
- The case must not be used to imply that coaches as a group are untrustworthy.

### 3.4 FIFPRO — contract and agent guidance
URL: https://www.fifpro.org/en/what-we-do/contract-guidance

FIFPRO advises players to retain a signed original contract, check terms carefully and seek advice from a player union or independent lawyer. It warns that an agent's incentives may not always align with the player if the relationship is not independent and long-standing.

Implication: ScoutSA should make relevant relationships and role claims clear, but must not present itself as a substitute for legal advice or a players' union.

## 4. What the player evidence supports — and what it does not

### Supported as risk-aware design inputs
- Show the specific organization, venue, date and responsible contact for an opportunity.
- Encourage independent confirmation through official club/organization channels.
- Make costs and responsibility for transport/accommodation explicit.
- Clarify what happens if an opportunity is cancelled, unsuccessful or the player is injured.
- Do not require payment to ScoutSA as a condition of being discoverable or considered.
- Minimize sensitive personal information and avoid public disclosure of a minor's contact details.
- Provide a way to flag suspicious offers and record the response without declaring an uninvestigated allegation to be a finding.
- Keep a record of request, response and outcome; do not imply that a request equals an invitation or placement.

### Not established
- That all agents or trials are fraudulent.
- That all upfront costs are fraudulent in every context.
- That the same risk rates apply to South African grassroots players.
- That transport is the dominant access barrier in the Vaal Triangle.
- That ScoutSA can solve equipment shortages, coaching shortages, transport or lack of competitions.
- That a digital profile increases selection or placement rates.
- That a reported allegation is a legal finding.

## 5. Product hypotheses to reconcile with prior research

P1. **Discovery and practical access are distinct.** A player may be visible to a recruiter but unable to attend; a player may be able to attend but remain undiscovered.

P2. **A genuine opportunity needs operational detail.** A club name alone is insufficient; the receiving organization should be able to confirm the opportunity and its terms.

P3. **Opportunity readiness should not become a proxy for talent.** A transport or guardian constraint should not automatically downgrade football ability.

P4. **Cost transparency matters.** Players need to know who pays for travel, accommodation, trial participation and other arrangements before committing.

P5. **Safeguarding and contingency matter.** Youth participation requires appropriate guardian/consent safeguards, a safe contact pathway and clear responsibility.

P6. **A reporting channel needs due process.** A concern should be recorded and responded to without automatically treating an allegation as proven or automatically excluding someone without an authorized process.

P7. **Existing networks may still be essential.** Coach referrals and personal introductions can help players; the system should add independent verification, not simply prohibit relationship-based discovery.

These remain hypotheses for ScoutSA, not measured Vaal Triangle facts.

## 6. Research gaps remaining

- Direct first-person accounts from Vaal Triangle grassroots players.
- Whether transport, fees, equipment, competition access, guardian availability, information trust or another factor most often blocks local opportunities.
- How minors and guardians currently receive, verify and accept trial invitations.
- What players consider acceptable data sharing and profile visibility.
- Whether coaches/clubs would confirm opportunities through an independent platform.
- Current local player-association, school, LFA and academy routes that already address these issues.

## Research conclusion

Player accounts reinforce that a football opportunity is more than a listing. It needs to be genuine, verifiable, clear about costs and logistics, and safe to pursue. They also caution against reducing the problem to exposure alone: equipment, coaching, transport, competition and support can matter.

ScoutSA can responsibly design for verification, transparency, purpose-limited disclosure and accurate outcome tracking. It cannot claim that these features solve the Vaal Triangle's principal barrier or guarantee selection. The local bottleneck remains unmeasured.
