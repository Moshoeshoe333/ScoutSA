# Part A — One-Page ScoutSA Explanation

## What ScoutSA is

ScoutSA is being designed as a **verified grassroots football opportunity network for South Africa**.

The starting focus is the Vaal Triangle.

The basic idea is simple:

> Help promising players become easier to discover, easier to verify, and easier to connect with genuine football opportunities — while handling youth participation, guardianship, consent and safeguarding carefully.

ScoutSA is **not** intended to be:

- a transfer marketplace;
- a public directory of children's contact details;
- an unrestricted social network;
- a talent-ranking system;
- an AI system that decides whether a player is good enough;
- a guarantee of trials, selection or placement.

## The problem we are testing

Finding a talented young player is only one part of the problem.

We are testing whether there is a second problem:

> **Even when a player has ability, what prevents that player from realistically reaching a genuine opportunity?**

Possible barriers might include transport, guardian availability, cost, school commitments, communication, lack of trustworthy information, safeguarding concerns, or something else entirely.

**We do not assume these are the barriers. We want stakeholders to tell us.**

## What a future ScoutSA experience might do

A player could have a structured **Player Passport** containing appropriately verified information.

A legitimate football organization could publish an **Opportunity**, such as:

- trial;
- assessment;
- academy intake;
- development opportunity.

A player and/or appropriately authorized participant could express interest.

The system could help establish whether the opportunity is practically workable and record the resulting request or outcome.

For youth players, access to information and contact would be appropriately scoped around guardian, consent and safeguarding requirements.

## What we want to learn

We are not asking you to endorse ScoutSA.

We want to know:

1. How do you currently discover players?
2. How do you decide whether information about a player is trustworthy?
3. What prevents promising players from reaching opportunities?
4. What information do you actually need before considering a player?
5. What information should never be exposed unnecessarily?
6. What makes a trial request useful or frustrating?
7. What role do guardians actually play in your current process?
8. What safeguarding problems occur in practice?
9. What would make a system like this useful enough to use?
10. What would make you refuse to use it?

**If the ScoutSA idea is solving the wrong problem, we want to find that out.**

# Part C — Evidence Capture

Separate observations from interpretation.

## Direct observation

Record what the participant actually said or demonstrated.

## Interpretation

What might this mean for ScoutSA?

## Contradiction

Does anything contradict a current assumption?

Examples:

- Opportunity Readiness appears less important than expected.
- Transport is a major barrier.
- Guardian workflow is more complicated than the current model assumes.
- Coaches need less information than expected.
- Verification requires institutional relationships.
- Existing channels already solve the discovery problem.
- Safeguarding is a larger operational issue than expected.

## Confidence

- High — directly observed/repeated clearly
- Medium — plausible but limited evidence
- Low — interpretation or isolated comment

## Action

- No change
- Product question
- MVP revision candidate
- Legal/safeguarding question
- Requires corroboration
- Potential contradiction requiring internal review

# Part D — Anti-Bias Rules

1. Do not sell ScoutSA during discovery.
2. Do not lead participants toward transport/guardian/cost explanations.
3. Do not treat one participant as representative of the Vaal Triangle.
4. Do not convert opinion into fact.
5. Preserve contradictory feedback.
6. Do not disclose unnecessary participant or minor information.
7. Do not promise features, opportunities or outcomes.
8. Do not describe the current model as legally compliant.
9. Do not ask participants to approve the architecture.
10. Do not create a new internal gate for each interview.

# Part E — Initial Validation Decision Rule

After the first five conversations:

### Confirmed
The evidence supports an existing assumption.

### Revised
The evidence materially changes an assumption.

### Reduced
A proposed feature appears unnecessary or excessive.

### New constraint
A practical, legal or safeguarding requirement emerges.

### Contradicted
External evidence directly conflicts with a material ScoutSA assumption.

### Unresolved
Evidence is insufficient; seek targeted corroboration rather than inventing a conclusion.

**No outcome is predetermined.**

**External evidence is evidence input, not automatic ratification.**
