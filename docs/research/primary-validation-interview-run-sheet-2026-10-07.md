# ScoutSA Primary Validation Interview Run-Sheet

**Status:** FIELD OPERATING CONTROL — NOT RATIFICATION  
**Date:** 2026-10-07  
**Purpose:** Keep every first-contact interview short, neutral, comparable and evidence-first.

## Before the call

- Confirm stakeholder role and context.
- Confirm the conversation is research, not a product pitch.
- Ask permission to take notes.
- Ask permission before recording, if recording is considered.
- Do not collect unnecessary minor/personal information.
- Set a 25–30 minute hard stop.

## Opening — approximately 2 minutes

> Thank you for giving me your time. I don't want to put words in your mouth or convince you of anything. I'd like to understand what actually happens to promising young footballers in the Vaal from the moment they're noticed until they get a genuine opportunity — or don't.

## Five universal questions — approximately 15 minutes

### 1. Discovery
**Think of the last genuinely promising player you encountered. How did that player come to your attention?**

### 2. Trust
**What made you believe the information about that player was reliable?**

### 3. Progression
**What happened between first hearing about the player and the player getting an actual opportunity?**

### 4. Failure
**Where do promising players most often fall out of that process?**

### 5. Existing solution
**Who or what already helps solve that problem?**

## Neutral probes — use only when relevant

- What did you check first?
- Who introduced the player?
- Did you see the player live?
- Was registration checked?
- Was video useful?
- Was it an invitation, open trial, assessment, showcase, tournament or another pathway?
- What information did you actually need?
- What role did the guardian play?
- What practical barrier mattered?
- Did money matter?
- Did school/education matter?
- Have you encountered fake scouts, agents or trials?
- What existing system already handles this?

**Do not suggest an answer before the stakeholder supplies one.**

## Falsifier — approximately 2 minutes

> **What would make you decide you don't need something like ScoutSA?**

Record the answer exactly enough to preserve its meaning.

## Concept reaction — only if time remains and only after discovery

Briefly describe ScoutSA as a possible system for appropriately verified player information, legitimate opportunities, controlled contact and practical access context.

Then ask:

- What part would actually be useful?
- What part would be unnecessary?
- What would worry you?
- What would you need to trust it?
- Would you use it? Why or why not?

Do not ask the stakeholder to approve the architecture or promise adoption.

## Close — approximately 1 minute

Thank them. Ask whether they can point to another relevant stakeholder **without treating the referral itself as validation**.

## Same-day five-bullet capture

Immediately after the call, write five bullets before doing anything else:

1. **Discovery** — how the player came to attention.
2. **Trust** — what made information credible.
3. **Progression** — what happened toward the opportunity.
4. **Failure** — where/why the pathway stalled.
5. **Existing solution** — who/what already solves part of it.

Then complete the Primary Validation Witness Record.

## Evidence discipline

Keep these separate:

**RAW WITNESS → CONCRETE CASE → INTERPRETATION → ASSUMPTION IMPACT → DECISION**

Do not upgrade:
- positive sentiment into demand;
- a referral into validation;
- a profile view into an opportunity;
- a trial invitation into attendance;
- attendance into placement;
- one interview into Vaal-wide truth.

## Interview stop condition

Stop at approximately 25–30 minutes even if additional questions remain. A shorter high-quality witness is preferable to a pressured interview.

## Authority boundary

This run-sheet authorizes **conversation structure only**.

It does not authorize:
- Constitution changes;
- MVP changes;
- schema/RLS/migration work;
- production deployment;
- legal-compliance claims;
- safeguarding certification;
- partnership or investor claims.

> **The interview is successful when we learn something true, including that ScoutSA may be solving the wrong problem.**
