## 2. Evidence register

### E-ELFA-001 — SAFA regional and LFA governance structure

**Source:** SAFA Statutes, last amended by SAFA Ordinary Congress on 26 March 2022. The copy reviewed is hosted by the South African Sports Law Association:  
https://sasportslawassociation.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/SAFA-STATUTE-26-MARCH-2022-SANDTON-CONVENTION-CENTRE.pdf

**Relevant provisions:**
- Article 10.1.1 lists Sedibeng (Vereeniging) among Gauteng's 52 Regional Associations.
- Article 10.1.2 describes each Regional Member as a representative unit for amateur football in its region.
- Article 10.1.3 says the relevant regional member's constitution is to provide for recognition and representation of LFAs at local level and set out their powers.

**What this supports:** Sedibeng is explicitly named in the 2022 SAFA Statutes' regional list, and LFA recognition/powers are situated within the relevant regional constitution.

**What it does not establish:** The current office-bearers, current ELFA constitution, current LFA boundaries, present delegation instruments, or the current status of any particular ELFA decision.

**Evidence class:** Governance-document text; copy is hosted by a third-party organisation, not an official SAFA domain. Obtain an authenticated current copy from SAFA before legal or operational reliance.

### E-ELFA-002 — Appointment/election evidence for representatives

**Source:** Same 2022 SAFA Statutes copy, Article 22.2.

Article 22.2 says delegates must be accredited by the Member they represent, having been appointed or elected by that Member, and written proof of appointment or election must be provided on written request.

**What this supports:** Within the specific SAFA delegate context, representation is linked to appointment/election by the represented Member and documentary proof.

**What it does not establish:** That Article 22.2 directly governs every club, school, academy, foundation or independent tournament organiser. Do not universalise this provision beyond its scope.

**ScoutSA implication:** It is a strong governance precedent for seeking attributable appointment/delegation evidence, while the precise evidence standard for each organisation type remains to be researched.

### E-ELFA-003 — Proposed new competitions and delegated competition organisation

**Source:** Same 2022 SAFA Statutes copy.
- Article 39.4 assigns SAFA's Organising Committee for SAFA Competitions the function of examining and approving applications for proposed new competitions by Members to be played within SAFA's jurisdiction.
- Article 66.1 describes official competitions organised, coordinated and/or regulated by SAFA.
- Article 66.2 says the SAFA NEC may delegate to subordinate Leagues authority to organise competitions, subject to the stated non-interference and priority conditions.

**What this supports:** SAFA's formal competition system includes defined approval and delegation mechanisms.

**Critical scope limitation:** Article 39.4 refers to proposed new competitions **by Members** within SAFA's jurisdiction. It must not be paraphrased as proof that every informal or independent community football event requires this exact committee approval. The applicable rules for independent festivals, friendly events and events involving affiliated clubs remain to be confirmed.

### E-ELFA-004 — Competition rights and event media authorisation

**Source:** Same 2022 SAFA Statutes copy.
- Article 68.1 describes SAFA and its Members as original owners of rights emanating from competitions and other events under their respective jurisdiction, including financial, audiovisual, recording, reproduction, broadcasting, multimedia, marketing, promotional, emblem and copyright-related rights.
- Article 68.2 assigns the SAFA NEC decisions about utilisation of those rights.
- Article 69 states that SAFA and its Members are responsible for authorising distribution of image, sound and other data carriers for football matches and events under their respective jurisdiction.

**What this supports:** For events within SAFA/Member jurisdiction, competition rights and distribution authorisation are governed separately from the identity of the person arranging an event.

**What it does not establish:** The precise rights-holder or permission pathway for every community event, venue, participant image, child image, third-party brand, or independently organised event. Applicable law, contracts, consent and other rights must be separately assessed.

**ScoutSA implication:** Never infer publication/media permission from organiser identity, venue access, team affiliation or tournament submission authority.

## 3. TAD-003 adversarial scenarios added

### O-11 — Contested or invalidated organisational election

**Scenario:** Two individuals claim to represent an LFA or club; a prior election is credibly reported or formally notified as invalidated, and a subsequent election is reported.

**Expected handling:** Mark the affected authority claim DISPUTED or PENDING pending confirmation from the competent governing authority. Restrict actions dependent on the disputed authority. Preserve prior records and the chronology; do not overwrite history. Reconfirm effective date, scope and issuer of the replacement mandate.

**Evidence needed to close the case:** Original resolution/notice, relevant constitution and election provisions, congress minutes/results, and current confirmation from the competent authority.

### O-12 — Independent foundation organises an event involving affiliated teams

**Scenario:** A community foundation is reported as organising a tournament, while participating teams are affiliated to an LFA.

**Expected handling:** Keep the foundation as the claimed organiser and the clubs' affiliation as a separate fact. Do not infer that the foundation is itself an ELFA member, that the event is sanctioned, or that the foundation has media rights. Verify each authority/permission dimension separately.

**Evidence needed to close the case:** Foundation identity and governing/registration evidence where applicable; event-specific representative mandate; written confirmation from the relevant football authority about any required approval/sanctioning; venue permission; and applicable publication/consent permissions.

## 4. Design implications — proposed, not ratified

1. **Temporal authority:** Representative appointments and delegations need effective dates and, where applicable, expiry dates.
2. **Dispute-aware state:** A credible election challenge, invalidation or competing mandate must trigger review rather than silently preserving a green/verified state.
3. **Historical preservation:** Leadership changes and revocations should supersede current authority without erasing prior evidence or actions.
4. **Actor separation:** Event organiser, participating club, LFA affiliation, sanctioning body, venue controller and publication rights-holder are distinct claims/roles.
5. **Scope-bound permissions:** Authority to arrange or submit an event does not by itself prove authority to claim sanctioning, use a venue, publish media or process player information.
6. **Independent organiser inclusion:** Do not automatically exclude a foundation or community organiser solely because it is not the participating teams' LFA. Determine applicable rules and evidence before classifying the event.
7. **Source provenance:** Press reports are discovery leads and secondary evidence; official resolutions, constitutions, minutes and direct confirmations are required for authority-sensitive conclusions.

These are design implications for TAD-003 review, not approved domain changes or legal conclusions.
