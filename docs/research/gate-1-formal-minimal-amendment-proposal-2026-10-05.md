# ScoutSA Gate 1 — Formal Minimal Semantic Amendment Proposal

**Status:** Proposal — NOT RATIFIED  
**Date:** 2026-10-05  
**Gate:** Gate 1 — Domain Integrity  
**Basis:** Cases 01–14; cross-case synthesis; external/legal anchors; candidate amendment re-witness  
**Authority:** None until formally ratified

---

## 1. Purpose

This document converts the surviving Gate 1 candidate clauses into the smallest proposed semantic amendment set required to close the witnessed domain gaps.

It does **not** authorize:

- implementation;
- ERD/schema changes;
- RLS policy;
- API contracts;
- UI states;
- automated decision algorithms;
- legal compliance claims;
- a universal guardian precedence rule;
- an absolute historical-retention obligation.

The proposal is deliberately semantic. Its purpose is to give the Constitution and Domain Model a precise candidate target for final contradiction review and ratification.

---

## 2. Proposed Amendment A — Epistemic Object Separation

ScoutSA must distinguish:

**Assertion → Evidence → Verification → Derived State → Decision**

An actor's assertion does not become truth merely because the actor is authenticated, role-bearing, institutionally affiliated, or otherwise verified.

Evidence supports claims but does not automatically establish truth. Verification is a scoped trust assertion and does not automatically verify unrelated attributes. Derived state is an output of underlying records and rules, not an independent source of truth.

**Affected witnesses:** Cases 01, 10, 13.

---

## 3. Proposed Amendment B — Scoped Authority and Conflict Preservation

Authority must be evaluated against the relevant:

- subject;
- claim;
- scope;
- context;
- effective time;
- applicable legal or policy basis.

A role, relationship, verification level, recency, or institutional affiliation does not by itself establish universal authority.

Where legitimately authorised assertions are incompatible, ScoutSA must preserve the competing assertions and provenance until an applicable legal, court, policy, or otherwise authorised resolution determines the permitted action.

Conflict must not be silently converted into resolution.

Resolution must not silently rewrite the underlying assertions.

**Affected witnesses:** Cases 02, 08, 13.

**Boundary:** This proposal does not establish a universal Guardian precedence hierarchy.

---

## 4. Proposed Amendment C — Purpose-Bound Authorization

Authorization must be limited to the purpose, scope, context, and time for which it is valid.

Authorization for one activity or disclosure purpose must not silently become authorization for another.

Examples include, but are not limited to:

- opportunity/trial participation;
- contact;
- media sharing;
- publication;
- secondary use;
- profile visibility.

The final authorization taxonomy remains subject to product policy and applicable legal review.

**Affected witnesses:** Cases 03, 06, 07, 08, 11.

---

## 5. Proposed Amendment D — Temporal Applicability and Freshness

A fact, evidence item, verification, consent, or other record that was valid or supported at one time must not automatically be treated as current, applicable, or sufficient for a later decision.

Current applicability must be evaluated against the relevant claim, purpose, scope, context, evidence freshness, authority state, and applicable rules.

Historical state must remain reconstructable where lawful and appropriate, subject to applicable retention and deletion requirements.

Therefore:

**AUTHENTIC ≠ CURRENT ≠ APPLICABLE ≠ SUFFICIENT**

**Affected witnesses:** Cases 04, 05, 12, with temporal implications for Case 03.

---

## 6. Proposed Amendment E — Derived-State Dependency and Reassessment

A derived state must remain explainable by the material inputs, rule/context, and evaluation time that produced it.

If a material input is withdrawn, revoked, expires, becomes materially disputed, or otherwise ceases to support the current evaluation, the current applicability of the derived state must be reassessed.

A replacement input produces a new current evaluation. It must not retroactively rewrite the historical evaluation.

Derived state must never become a substitute for the underlying authoritative records.

**Affected witnesses:** Cases 05, 14, and Cases 04/12 where derived-state dependence is material.

**Boundary:** This defines semantic correctness only. It does not select triggers, jobs, event sourcing, recalculation architecture, or database mechanics.

---

## 7. Proposed Amendment F — Safeguarding Is Distinct From Verification

Verification of identity, role, organization, or football-related claims must not by itself be treated as safeguarding clearance or authorization to contact, disclose information to, or otherwise interact with a minor.

Safeguarding concerns require an applicable control, reporting, resolution, and audit pathway.

ScoutSA must not invent a universal legal definition of safeguarding clearance where applicable law or competent authority supplies the governing standard.

**Affected witnesses:** Cases 06 and 09.

---

## 8. Proposed Amendment G — Disclosure and Republication Boundary

Discoverability, public availability, or presence in an external record does not by itself establish that ScoutSA may reproduce, aggregate, transform, disclose, or reuse information for a new purpose.

Any public-information pathway must be evaluated against the applicable purpose, further-processing, child-data, authority, consent, safeguarding, and other legal constraints relevant to that use.

This amendment does not create a blanket prohibition on lawful public-information use.

**Affected witnesses:** Cases 11 and 12, with related implications for Case 07.

---

## 9. Proposed Cross-Domain Invariants

If ratified, the following invariants would become governing semantic constraints:

1. `ASSERTION ≠ EVIDENCE`
2. `EVIDENCE ≠ VERIFICATION`
3. `VERIFICATION ≠ TRUTH`
4. `ROLE ≠ UNIVERSAL AUTHORITY`
5. `AUTHORITY ≠ CONSENT`
6. `PURPOSE A AUTHORIZATION ≠ PURPOSE B AUTHORIZATION`
7. `HISTORICAL VALIDITY ≠ CURRENT APPLICABILITY`
8. `AUTHENTIC ≠ CURRENT ≠ APPLICABLE ≠ SUFFICIENT`
9. `CONFLICT ≠ RESOLUTION`
10. `DERIVED STATE ≠ SOURCE OF TRUTH`
11. `VERIFICATION ≠ SAFEGUARDING CLEARANCE`
12. `DISCOVERABLE ≠ REPUBLISHABLE`
13. `INPUT WITHDRAWAL ≠ HISTORY ERASURE`

These are proposed invariants only.

---

## 10. Explicit Non-Amendments

This proposal does **not** ratify or imply:

- a single Guardian as universally primary;
- latest-assertion-wins;
- strongest-verification-wins;
- role-based automatic precedence;
- automatic legal conclusions from repository state;
- a fixed consent enum;
- a fixed evidence-freshness duration;
- an absolute retention period;
- automatic deletion or invalidation mechanics;
- a safeguarding-clearance badge;
- public-data republication permission;
- AI authority;
- a compliance certification.

---

## 11. Ratification Test

Before any proposal is ratified, the repository must establish:

1. no contradiction with the Constitution;
2. no contradiction with the existing Domain Model;
3. no unsupported legal assumption;
4. no hidden implementation dependency;
5. no loss of provenance;
6. no silent conflict resolution;
7. no conversion of historical state into current authority;
8. no conversion of derived state into source-of-truth;
9. no broader claim than the evidence supports.

Only after this test may affected witnesses be re-witnessed against the formal proposal and Gate 1 be considered for transition.
