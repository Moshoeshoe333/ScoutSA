## 4. 33×3 synthesis

### Existence

The core vocabulary is strong: Player, Guardian/GuardianLink, ConsentRecord, Evidence, Verification, Media, Organization, Membership, Opportunity, OpportunityApplication, Opportunity Readiness, temporal state, provenance, visibility and audit/history concepts.

The recurring existence gaps are:

- **Assertion boundary:** Cases 1, 10 and 13 show that an actor statement is not identical to Evidence, Verification, Truth or Derived State. A complete first-class Assertion boundary is still missing.
- **Conflict state:** Cases 1, 2, 8, 9 and 13 show that legitimate disagreement can exist without a safe first-class conflict condition.
- **Derived dependency:** Cases 4, 5 and 14 show that point-in-time readiness needs explicit dependency/invalidation semantics.

### Coherence

The witnesses consistently support these principles:

1. `ROLE ≠ UNIVERSAL AUTHORITY`
2. `ASSERTION ≠ EVIDENCE`
3. `EVIDENCE ≠ TRUTH`
4. `VERIFICATION ≠ UNIVERSAL TRUST`
5. `CONSENT ≠ GENERAL PERMISSION`
6. `HISTORICAL VALIDITY ≠ CURRENT APPLICABILITY`
7. `AUTHENTIC ≠ CURRENT ≠ APPLICABLE ≠ SUFFICIENT`
8. `DERIVED STATE ≠ SOURCE OF TRUTH`
9. `CONFLICT ≠ RESOLUTION`
10. `DISCOVERABLE ≠ REPUBLISHABLE`

### Failure

Four recurring failure classes appear across the fourteen witnesses:

**F-A — Silent promotion:** assertion becomes truth; role becomes authority; verification becomes universal trust; trial consent becomes media permission; historical state becomes current authorization; public availability becomes republication authority; readiness becomes current authorization.

**F-B — Silent invalidation:** historical consent, evidence, verification or readiness is deleted or rewritten after later change.

**F-C — Silent conflict resolution:** latest assertion, strongest-looking role, verified organization, Boolean state or AI inference is used to manufacture a winner.

**F-D — Provenance loss:** a current answer cannot be traced through subject, claim, authority, purpose, scope, time, evidence, verification, derived state, decision, disclosure and audit.

## 5. Seven orthogonal semantic gaps

### G1 — Assertion Boundary
Cases 1, 10, 13.
Actor statement → Assertion → supporting Evidence → Verification → Derived State.
**Status: candidate; not ratified.**

### G2 — Scoped Authority and Conflict
Cases 2, 8, 13.
Authority must be evaluated against subject, claim, scope, context and time. Multiple legitimate authorities do not create an automatic winner.
**Status: candidate; not ratified.**

### G3 — Purpose-Bound Authorization
Cases 3, 6, 7, 8.
Trial, contact, media, publication, disclosure and secondary-use permissions must not silently expand into one another.
**Status: candidate; not ratified.**

### G4 — Temporal Freshness and Applicability
Cases 3, 4, 5, 12.
Historical validity does not establish current applicability. Freshness must be evaluated against the claim and context.
**Status: candidate; not ratified.**

### G5 — Derived-State Dependency and Invalidation
Cases 4, 5, 12, 14.
Material inputs feed a point-in-time derived state. Material withdrawal or change must constrain current state while preserving the historical evaluation.
**Status: candidate; not ratified.**

### G6 — Safeguarding vs Verification
Cases 6 and 9.
Identity verification, organization verification, Scout verification, safeguarding clearance and contact authorization are distinct controls.
**Status: candidate; not ratified.**

### G7 — Disclosure / Republication Boundary
Cases 7 and 11, supported by 6 and 12.
Public discovery does not automatically establish authority to disclose, republish, aggregate, transform or use information for a new purpose.
**Status: candidate; external evidence required before formalization.**

## 6. Minimal candidate amendment set

The synthesis does not recommend seven separate amendments. A smaller future set may be sufficient:

- **M1 — Epistemic Object Separation:** distinguish Assertion, Evidence, Verification, Derived State and Decision.
- **M2 — Scoped Authority and Conflict Preservation:** authority is claim/subject/scope/context/time bound; legitimate conflict remains representable.
- **M3 — Purpose / Authorization Separation:** authorization for one purpose cannot silently expand to another.
- **M4 — Temporal Applicability:** historical validity and current applicability remain distinct; freshness is claim/context dependent.
- **M5 — Derived-State Dependency:** material input changes constrain current derived state while historical evaluations remain reconstructable.
- **M6 — Safeguarding / Disclosure Boundary:** verification, safeguarding, contact authorization, visibility, republication and secondary use remain distinct controls.

These are candidate themes only. No Constitution or Domain Model text is ratified by this synthesis.

## 7. What remains strong

The synthesis finds no reason to weaken the existing Constitution. The following should remain intact:

- Strength of claim must never exceed strength of evidence.
- Trust over growth.
- Guardian control for minors, subject to applicable authority and law.
- Opportunity Readiness is contextual, not a permanent Player score.
- Verification is scoped.
- Historical relationships and records are preserved.
- Sensitive information remains purpose- and visibility-controlled.
- Documentation does not prove implementation.
- Legal compliance is not claimed merely because documentation says it is required.
