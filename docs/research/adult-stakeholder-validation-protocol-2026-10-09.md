**Prepared:** 2026-10-09  
**Status:** RESEARCH PLAN — NOT EXECUTED; no stakeholder responses recorded  
**AuthorityLevel:** NONE to change product/schema policy or authorize live use  
**Scope:** Adult football stakeholders only; initial Vaal Triangle discovery  
**Related records:** Trust Core gap-boundary reconciliation; gap-disposition proposal; ScoutSA constitution and MVP boundary.

## 1. Research contract

**Decision this research should inform:** Which Trust Core requirements are genuine operational prerequisites for a trustworthy grassroots football opportunity workflow, which are already represented by existing concepts, which can be deferred from v1, and which require separate policy/legal decisions.

**As-of date:** 2026-10-09.

**Population:** Adults acting in professional or volunteer football roles in the Vaal Triangle/Sedibeng area: club chairpersons/managers, senior coaches, academy administrators, adult scouts/talent identifiers, and where useful adult player-support or transport coordinators. Do not interview minors for this protocol.

**Initial sample target:** 5–8 adult stakeholders across at least three distinct role categories and at least two organizations. This is a qualitative discovery target, not a statistically representative sample.

**Evidence lanes:**
1. Direct interview evidence: only notes recorded after a real conversation, with date, role category, consent to note-taking, exact question, answer/paraphrase clearly labelled, and limitations.
2. Documentary evidence: official club/association rules, registration requirements, published trial notices, fee schedules, safeguarding or consent procedures. Save title, publisher, URL, publication/update date where available, access date, exact relevant passage, and limitation.
3. Counterevidence: actively ask what would make a proposed rule unnecessary, impractical, or harmful; record dissent and examples where a requirement is not used in practice.

Search snippets and publicly visible contact details are leads, not interview evidence or proof of authority.

**Stop condition:** Stop discovery when each high-priority gap has either (a) corroboration from two independent adult roles/organizations, (b) a documented disagreement requiring a product decision, or (c) an explicit evidence gap that cannot yet be resolved. Do not convert a small qualitative sample into a claim of market-wide validation.

## 2. Guardrails

- Adult stakeholders only for this round. Do not recruit, contact, profile, or collect data about minors.
- Do not request personal player records, identity documents, medical information, guardian details, private scouting reports, or live opportunity credentials.
- Do not ask stakeholders to send real player data or upload documents to ScoutSA.
- Explain that ScoutSA is in research/design validation, not a live service; do not promise listings, trials, placements, funding, or access to scouts.
- Participation is voluntary. Ask permission before taking notes or attributing a quote. Prefer role-only attribution unless the participant explicitly agrees to named attribution.
- Do not record calls unless separately and explicitly agreed. No covert recording.
- Separate reported practice from independently verified fact.
- Do not treat any interview as legal advice, safeguarding clearance, product ratification, or permission to launch.
- No outreach is sent by this document. Contacting any organization/person requires a separate user-directed action.

## 3. Neutral introduction

“I'm researching how grassroots football opportunities are communicated and checked in the Vaal Triangle. ScoutSA is a proposed opportunity network intended to help people distinguish a genuine opportunity from one that is actually suitable and feasible for a particular player. It is not live, and we are not collecting player records or recruiting players. I would value your adult professional perspective on how this works today, where it fails, and what evidence would make an opportunity trustworthy. There are no right answers; critical feedback is useful.”

## 4. Interview guide (20–30 minutes)

### A. Role and current process
1. What is your role in local football, and which decisions do you personally make?
2. Walk me through the last time your club/organization shared or assessed a trial, recruitment, academy, tournament, or development opportunity.
3. Who was responsible for confirming that the opportunity and the person publishing it were genuine?
4. What evidence did you actually check? What did you rely on without checking?
5. How did you know the opportunity was still open and had capacity?

### B. Conditions for a real opportunity
6. Before you would describe an opportunity as “verified,” what minimum facts must be confirmed?
7. Which of these are essential in your context: responsible organization/contact, date/time, venue, eligibility, age group, capacity, costs/fees and recipient, transport/access, required equipment/documents, deadline, and cancellation/contact route? Why?
8. Which facts change often enough that they need re-confirmation? Who should set the re-confirmation date and on what basis?
9. What should happen if the source is genuine but capacity, eligibility, venue, cost, or date is still unknown?
10. Is there a meaningful distinction between “the organization is genuine,” “the opportunity conditions were checked,” and “this is actionable for a particular participant”? Please give an example or explain why not.

### C. Trust failures and counterexamples
11. What are the most common misleading, stale, incomplete, or unfair opportunity claims you encounter?
12. Have you seen an authentic club or scout associated with a false, outdated, or misleading opportunity? Describe the pattern without naming or exposing individuals.
13. What would make a verification badge misleading or dangerous?
14. Which proposed checks would create unnecessary work, exclude small/community clubs, or produce false confidence?
15. What is the strongest counterexample to the proposed rule that missing material conditions should block participant-specific actionability?

### D. Feasibility and fairness
16. When does transport, venue distance, time, cost, equipment, school/work obligations, or disability/accessibility change whether an opportunity is realistic?
17. Who normally confirms those conditions, and what is safe to ask a participant versus an organization?
18. How should a platform represent “ready with constraints” without exposing sensitive personal circumstances?
19. What costs should be disclosed, and what would make a fee demand suspicious? Do not share bank details or private cases.

### E. Consent, correction and accountability (high-level only)
20. In your role, what permissions or authorizations would normally be needed before sharing a young person's profile or contact details? Please distinguish your own practice from legal advice.
21. If a verification is wrong or someone disputes it, who should be able to request correction, and what history should remain visible to authorized reviewers?
22. What should the system do when consent is missing, withdrawn, or does not cover the intended use?
23. Who should be able to see a verification, and who should not?

### F. Prioritization
24. Of the requirements discussed, which three must be solved before a platform could safely show an opportunity as actionable?
25. Which can be deferred from an initial MVP without misleading users?
26. What evidence or rule have I failed to ask about?
27. May we follow up with a short factual clarification? May we attribute any statement to your role or organization?

## 7. Synthesis rule

For every product requirement, classify the evidence as one of:
- **CORROBORATED PRACTICE** — supported by independent direct accounts and/or documentary evidence, with provenance retained;
- **SINGLE-SOURCE REPORT** — useful lead, not enough to generalize;
- **CONTESTED** — credible stakeholders disagree; preserve the disagreement;
- **DESIGN HYPOTHESIS** — our interpretation awaiting evidence;
- **POLICY/LEGAL HOLD** — cannot be decided by stakeholder preference alone;
- **UNRESOLVED** — evidence insufficient.

Do not count repeated claims from the same source as independent corroboration. Do not convert a stakeholder preference into law or an implementation authorization.
