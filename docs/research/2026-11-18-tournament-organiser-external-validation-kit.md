# ScoutSA Tournament Organiser — External Validation Kit

**Prepared:** 18 November 2026  
**Status:** RESEARCH INSTRUMENT — NOT PRODUCT AUTHORIZATION  
**Scope:** Vaal Triangle / Sedibeng tournament and grassroots competition operations  
**Decision under investigation:** Is a narrow tournament/team-level administration workflow a real, recurring problem worth prioritising in ScoutSA?

## 1. Research contract

**Question.** How do local organisers currently create competitions, admit teams, schedule fixtures, record results, resolve disputes/corrections, and preserve records?

**Decision this research may inform.** Whether to retain, narrow, redesign, defer, or reject the proposed Tournament Organiser workflow as an MVP priority.

**Out of scope.** Proving market size; validating the whole ScoutSA thesis; collecting player profiles; testing production software; obtaining legal approval; authorising schema, API, UI, RLS, migrations, authentication, deployment, or a pilot.

**Evidence rule.** Interview statements are attributed reports, not verified facts about every organisation. Prefer observed artefacts (blank forms, blank team sheets, fixture templates, anonymised result records, written rules) over recollection. Do not copy or retain personal/player data from supplied examples.

## 2. Who to speak to

Aim for **5–8 exploratory interviews**, if access permits. This is a discovery sample, not a statistically representative survey. Seek different roles rather than eight people from one organisation:

1. Tournament organiser / local tournament committee member.
2. League administrator or competition secretary.
3. Club/team administrator who submits entries and results.
4. Referee or match official who records match events/results.
5. Development-league or school-sport coordinator.
6. Where feasible, an independent safeguarding/privacy practitioner for a separate review.

## 3. Interview protocol (30–40 minutes)

### Opening and consent (2 minutes)

> Thank you for considering a short research conversation. We are studying how grassroots football competitions are actually administered. This is not a sales pitch and we are not asking you to endorse ScoutSA. We will not collect player names, identity numbers, dates of birth, contact details, photographs, medical information, or safeguarding case details. May I take written notes? May I quote you anonymously, or would you prefer that your comments remain unattributed?

Record consent choice. If permission to take notes or use the information is declined, do not record it. No audio recording unless separately and explicitly agreed.

### A. Recent real example (5 minutes)

1. Please walk me through the most recent tournament or competition you helped administer, from planning to final results.
2. What was your role, and which tasks did you personally perform?
3. What records or documents did you use at each stage?
4. Could you show a blank or fully anonymised example of a form/template? We do not need completed records containing personal information.

**Probe:** What happened in the last competition, not what ideally should happen?

### B. Team entry and organiser authority (5 minutes)

5. How do teams apply or get admitted? Who checks that the team is eligible?
6. What information is essential at team-entry stage, and what is collected later by someone else?
7. How do you distinguish the tournament organiser, the team representative, the club, the league, and any governing association?
8. What happens when someone claims authority to act for a team or association but that authority is disputed?

### C. Fixtures, results and corrections (8 minutes)

9. Who creates and communicates fixtures? How are postponements or venue/time changes handled?
10. Who records the result, and what is the accepted source of record?
11. Can you describe the most recent disagreement about a result, fixture, team entry, or eligibility? What happened step by step?
12. Who is allowed to correct a published result? Is the original retained? Is a reason recorded?
13. What happens when two people submit different scores or records?
14. Are match reports, referee reports, team sheets, or other records used? Which ones matter for which decisions?

### D. Devices, connectivity and workload (5 minutes)

15. Which devices and tools are used on match day (paper, WhatsApp, spreadsheets, league system, phone, laptop)?
16. Describe a recent occasion when connectivity, battery, device access, or data cost disrupted the work.
17. What did you do at the time? What information was lost, duplicated, delayed, or corrected later?
18. Would offline capture solve a real recurring problem, or would it introduce more risk/work? Why?

### E. Data minimisation and safeguarding boundary (5 minutes)

19. Which records can be managed using tournament/team information only?
20. At what point, if any, do individual player details become necessary, and who is responsible for that process today?
21. Which information should a tournament organiser never be able to see or change?
22. What safeguards or approvals are required before information about under-18 players is collected or shared?

Do not solicit examples containing identifiable child information. If the participant begins sharing such details, interrupt politely and ask them to describe the process without names or identifying specifics.

### F. Value and disconfirmation (5 minutes)

23. Which part of the current process consumes the most time or creates the most errors?
24. What tool or existing process already solves this well?
25. If you could remove one administrative task, what would it be?
26. What would make a new system unusable or not worth adopting?
27. If ScoutSA only helped manage tournament identity, team entries, fixtures, result submissions and correction history — with no player profiles or scouting features — would that solve a problem you have actually experienced? What is the strongest reason it might not?

**Close:** May we contact you once to clarify a point? May we request a blank template or public competition rule? Thank them; do not promise features, timelines, partnerships, or access.

## 5. Pre-committed interpretation and falsification

Do not count enthusiasm as proof of demand. Look for repeated, concrete, recent examples and inspectable evidence.

### Evidence that would strengthen the workflow hypothesis

- Multiple independent organisations describe substantially similar recurring administrative friction.
- The same minimum workflow is evidenced by actual forms, templates, rules, or records.
- Organisers can identify who has authority to submit, acknowledge, dispute, correct, and accept records.
- Team/tournament-level data solves a meaningful task without needing player personal data.
- A narrow tool fits existing match-day practice better than current tools, with credible adoption conditions.

### Evidence that would weaken or falsify it

- Existing league systems already solve the relevant workflow adequately.
- Problems are rare, low-cost, or handled more reliably through existing paper/WhatsApp processes.
- Organisers do not want another system or cannot sustain the required administration.
- Authority to accept results is too fragmented or unclear for a safe common workflow.
- The useful task cannot be separated from sensitive player data, and no justified safeguarding/privacy design exists.
- Offline support is assumed but no recurring, material connectivity failure is evidenced.
- Different competition types require incompatible workflows, making the proposed generic MVP misleading.

### Decision rule

After 5–8 interviews (or earlier if access is exhausted), prepare a short evidence synthesis:

- **Retain:** recurring pain and coherent narrow workflow evidenced across independent roles.
- **Narrow:** only one sub-workflow is consistently valuable.
- **Redesign:** current domain assumptions conflict with actual practice.
- **Defer:** evidence is mixed, access is insufficient, or a prerequisite is unresolved.
- **Reject:** existing processes are adequate or the proposed workflow does not solve a material problem.

These are research dispositions, not governance ratification. Record sample limitations and dissenting evidence. Do not convert a small qualitative sample into a market-wide claim.
