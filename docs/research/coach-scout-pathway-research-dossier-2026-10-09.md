# ScoutSA Research Dossier 02 — Coaches, Scouts and Youth Player Pathways
**Research date:** 2026-10-09  
**Status:** Desk research; not direct Vaal Triangle validation  
**Decision supported:** Understand how players are identified and assessed, where pathways break down, and what information ScoutSA might responsibly help organize.  
**Evidence lanes:** Primary/institutional interviews; peer-reviewed research; media reporting. Claims are not treated as local measurements unless the source directly supports that scope.

## 1. Executive findings

1. **South African football leaders have publicly described a fragmented or underdeveloped talent pipeline.** In an October 2022 TimesLIVE interview, SAFA technical director Walter Steenbok discussed the need for regional scouting structures, school football, interprovincial tournaments, better data and standardized selection criteria. He said SAFA did not have an answer to how many academies operated nationally at that time. This is a dated account of the challenges and plans he described then, not proof that the same state persists unchanged in 2026. [TimesLIVE interview](https://www.timeslive.co.za/sport/soccer/2022-10-12-full-interview-safa-td-steenbok-reveals-plans-to-rescue-sa-football/)
2. **Grassroots resourcing is a live public concern.** In an EWN report dated 18 July 2025, FIFA Regional Technical Consultant Serame Letsoaka argued that investment was concentrated at higher levels and that grassroots, school and street football lacked support. Transnet Football School of Excellence manager Mojalefa Mathebula also highlighted the need for a platform to discuss youth-football developments. These are practitioner views, not a quantified national audit. [EWN](https://www.ewn.co.za/sa-coaches-on-true-state-of-football-development-in-sa-we-have-talent-but-we-need-resources/)
3. **Scouting is not a perfectly objective measurement process.** A FIFA Training Centre research brief summarizes a survey of 125 Netherlands-based scouts. It reports that scouts combine structured criteria with subjective judgment; some found it difficult to define technical ability precisely, and many felt reliable prediction of potential in very young players was difficult. The brief warns against excessive reliance on physical attributes and calls for clearer criteria. [FIFA Training Centre](https://www.fifatrainingcentre.com/en/environment/research-brief/high-performance/find/bergkamp---how-scouts-assess-players.php)
4. **A South African study of nine scouts found four broad assessment dimensions:** technical, physical, psychosocial and tactical attributes. Examples included first touch, dribbling, passing, finishing, height/speed/endurance, discipline/coachability, decision-making and game awareness. This study supports the multidimensional nature of assessment; its small sample does not establish universal weighting or a validated scoring formula. [University of Johannesburg research record](https://pure.uj.ac.za/en/publications/a-qualitative-study-of-talent-identification-and-selection-in-you/)
5. **Pathways are plural.** The South African eThekwini coach study (13 coaches from five youth development clubs) described open trials, word of mouth, telephone inquiries, club websites, coach referrals and scouting as routes into clubs. It also reported concerns about facilities, funding, leadership, age-cheating and selection. It is older and geographically bounded; it does not measure current Vaal Triangle conditions. [ResearchGate record / study details](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/292150402_Coaches%27_perspectives_on_youth_football_development_programmes_in_the_eThekwini_region_KwaZulu-Natal_South_Africa)
6. **The current South African U15 coach has emphasized technical ability and effective play within the team.** In a December 2023 FIFA Training Centre interview, Vela Khumalo described a national scouting network and the U15 pathway; he stressed coaching players to use technical ability effectively in possession and balancing individual and collective play. [FIFA interview](https://www.fifatrainingcentre.com/en/practice/elite-sessions/youth-national-teams/south-africa-u15/vela-khumalo-on-laying-the-foundations-for-the-next-generation.php)
7. **ScoutSA should not claim it can predict talent.** Research supports organizing evidence and opportunity access, but it does not support a single universal talent score, automated prediction or a guarantee that a profile will lead to selection.

## 2. Primary / institutional lane

### 2.1 SAFA technical director Walter Steenbok — TimesLIVE interview (12 October 2022)

URL: https://www.timeslive.co.za/sport/soccer/2022-10-12-full-interview-safa-td-steenbok-reveals-plans-to-rescue-sa-football/

Relevant passages/claims in the article:
- The technical director described the talent pipeline as a problem and discussed establishing scouting structures in regions.
- He emphasized school football and interprovincial tournaments as important sources of players and development.
- He said scouting is a distinct skill and argued for standardized selection criteria and training.
- He described the academy ecosystem as “haphazard” at the time and said SAFA did not have a count of academies.
- He discussed tracking players, recording why players are dropped, and avoiding losing late developers.

Interpretation:
- A pathway is not simply a public player profile. It involves repeated observation, development, competition, regional coverage, selection criteria and follow-up.
- Data can help only if the system has clear collection practices, appropriate coverage, and people who act on it.

Limitations:
- Interview is from 2022 and reports a leader's diagnosis and plans, not an independent evaluation of outcomes.
- Do not state that SAFA currently lacks an academy database or that these 2022 plans remain unimplemented without current evidence.

### 2.2 EWN — South African coaches on grassroots resources (18 July 2025)

URL: https://www.ewn.co.za/sa-coaches-on-true-state-of-football-development-in-sa-we-have-talent-but-we-need-resources/

EWN reports comments from Serame Letsoaka, FIFA Regional Technical Consultant, and Mojalefa Mathebula, Transnet Football School of Excellence manager. Letsoaka argued that investment was concentrated at the top levels and that grassroots, school sport and street football were under-supported. Mathebula said youth football knowledge and activity lacked a sufficiently visible platform for discussion.

Interpretation:
- There may be an information-sharing gap, but the article does not show that a digital platform is the preferred solution.
- Funding, facilities, coaching and competition availability are structural issues; a discovery tool cannot substitute for them.

Limitations:
- Interview/reporting, not a representative survey or quantified funding analysis.
- The remarks are attributed opinions and should be described as such.

### 2.3 FIFA Training Centre — Vela Khumalo, South Africa U15 head coach (5 December 2023)

URL: https://www.fifatrainingcentre.com/en/practice/elite-sessions/youth-national-teams/south-africa-u15/vela-khumalo-on-laying-the-foundations-for-the-next-generation.php

The interview discusses the U15 national pathway, talent identification and national football philosophy. The article says Khumalo described SAFA's regional scouting presence, the aim of introducing players to national-team principles earlier, technical ability as a key consideration, and the need to coach natural ability into effective possession and collective play.

Interpretation:
- ScoutSA should support context-rich evidence, not reduce players to highlight clips or raw metrics.
- “Ability” should be distinguished from development, tactical understanding, readiness for a specific opportunity and practical ability to attend.

Limitations:
- Institutional interview; it is not a full recruitment manual or an independent audit of the national scouting network.

## 3. Research / independent evidence lane

### 3.1 FIFA Training Centre research brief — “How scouts assess players” (31 August 2023)

URL: https://www.fifatrainingcentre.com/en/environment/research-brief/high-performance/find/bergkamp---how-scouts-assess-players.php

Author: Dr Tom Bergkamp, researcher at the Royal Netherlands Football Association (KNVB). The brief summarizes a survey of 125 scouts based in the Netherlands and cites the paper “How soccer scouts identify talented players,” European Journal of Sport Science, 2022, 22(7), 994–1004.

Key findings summarized by FIFA:
- Scouts often mix structured criteria with holistic or intuitive judgments.
- Many struggle to define criteria such as “technical ability” in precise terms.
- Some scouts believe it is difficult to judge future potential reliably until mid-to-late adolescence.
- Heavy emphasis on physical attributes can introduce selection bias.
- More explicit and reliable assessment criteria warrant further research.

Interpretation:
- A ScoutSA “readiness” label must not be confused with a prediction of future talent.
- Any structured profile should expose source/context and uncertainty; avoid a universal automated score.
- Different roles, age groups and opportunities may legitimately require different assessment criteria.

Limitations:
- Netherlands-based sample; not direct evidence about South African coaches.
- A research brief summarizes a study; it is not the complete paper.

### 3.2 South African qualitative study — talent scouts' perceptions

Institutional record: University of Johannesburg.  
URL: https://pure.uj.ac.za/en/publications/a-qualitative-study-of-talent-identification-and-selection-in-you/

Authors: Walter Steenbok, Heather Morris-Eyton, Alliance Kubayi. Published in the *Journal of Sports Medicine and Physical Fitness*, volume 65, issue 12. The institutional abstract reports interviews with nine scouts and four themes:
- technical: first touch, dribbling, passing, finishing;
- physical: height, speed, endurance;
- psychosocial: discipline, coachability;
- tactical: decision-making, game sense and awareness.

Interpretation:
- A credible Player Passport could organize verified evidence and context across dimensions, but should not assert that every coach weights them equally.
- The profile should not stigmatize late developers or treat physical maturity as a proxy for long-term potential.

Limitations:
- Nine interviewees; qualitative study.
- Abstract reports themes, not validated weights, prediction accuracy or universal selection rules.
- Full text and publication date should be confirmed before deeper methodological claims.

### 3.3 Coaches' perspectives on youth football development in eThekwini (2015)

URL: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/292150402_Coaches%27_perspectives_on_youth_football_development_programmes_in_the_eThekwini_region_KwaZulu-Natal_South_Africa

Article details in the available record: J. Davids, R. Naidoo and Y. Coopoo; *African Journal for Physical, Health Education, Recreation and Dance*, November 2015 supplement, pp. 236–250. The study interviewed 13 coaches across five youth development clubs.

Reported themes include dissatisfaction around leadership, facilities, financial support, age-cheating, selection processes and coaching qualifications. The report says clubs used open trials and players also gained access through word of mouth, telephone inquiries, websites, coach referrals and scouting. It describes concerns about the consistency and fairness of selection, alongside views that scouting requires skill and experience.

Interpretation:
- Existing channels already serve some discovery needs; ScoutSA must complement, not assume it replaces, them.
- The product question is whether it can improve reliability, access, verification or follow-through in a way that existing networks do not.
- Selection fairness is a potential issue to investigate, not a conclusion that all selection is biased.

Limitations:
- Older study, small sample and eThekwini/KZN setting.
- The source page is a secondary hosting record; primary publication details should be checked where possible.
- Do not generalize to all South African clubs or the Vaal Triangle.

## 4. Evidence synthesis: what selection appears to involve

Across the reviewed sources, the recurring dimensions are:

| Dimension | Examples in sources | ScoutSA implication |
|---|---|---|
| Technical | First touch, passing, dribbling, finishing, effective possession | Capture evidence and context; avoid highlight-only claims |
| Tactical | Decisions, game awareness, positioning, team contribution | Preserve position, match context and observer perspective |
| Physical | Speed, endurance, height/maturity | Avoid treating physical maturity as universal talent or potential |
| Psychosocial | Discipline, coachability, response to instruction | Attribute observations to a source and context; avoid unsupported labels |
| Development | Training, age/stage, repeated observation, late development | Allow updated evidence and historical trajectory; do not freeze a child in one assessment |
| Opportunity-specific fit | Role, team need, level, eligibility, timing | Distinguish “football ability” from fit for one particular opportunity |
| Practical access | Attendance, travel, costs, schedule, guardian arrangements | Treat as a separate opportunity-access question, not as a talent judgment |

This table is a synthesis, not an official universal scouting rubric.

## 5. Research hypotheses to test against player accounts next

H1. Discovery relies partly on local networks, coach referrals, competitions and trials; digital visibility alone may not create trust or access.

H2. Players can be missed when regional coverage, competition exposure, data and follow-up are inconsistent. Evidence: public South African football leadership statements, particularly the 2022 interview; current magnitude remains unknown.

H3. Scout evaluations combine structured criteria and subjective judgment, so ScoutSA must avoid presenting one score as objective truth.

H4. A useful profile is likely to require contextual evidence rather than self-asserted attributes or highlight reels alone.

H5. Opportunity access and football ability should remain separate. A player who cannot attend a trial for practical reasons should not automatically be labelled less talented or less ready to play.

H6. The platform must coexist with existing coach networks, school football, local football associations, club websites, open trials and scouting.

These are hypotheses, not findings proven for the Vaal Triangle.

## 6. What remains unknown

- Current state and coverage of grassroots scouting in the Vaal Triangle.
- Which channels are most used by local coaches and recruiters in 2026.
- Whether coaches would use a digital Player Passport and which evidence they would trust.
- Whether local selection practices are consistent or fair.
- Whether practical access constraints materially prevent players from attending trials.
- Whether clubs/academies will publish genuine opportunities on an independent platform.
- Whether ScoutSA would improve outcomes compared with existing networks.

## 7. Product implications — provisional

1. **Evidence before claims:** distinguish self-assertion, coach observation, competition record, official record and verification.
2. **Context before scoring:** record age band, position, competition level, date, observer and match/training context where relevant.
3. **No automated talent verdict:** do not build a universal ranking or prediction score from this research.
4. **Separate readiness dimensions:** football readiness is not opportunity readiness, and neither is a guarantee of selection.
5. **Respect development over time:** allow evidence to be updated; avoid permanent negative labels from one assessment.
6. **Complement existing channels:** support referrals and opportunity workflows instead of pretending to replace coach relationships or local competitions.
7. **Don't confuse visibility with access:** a profile can be seen without a coach being interested, a trial being available, or a player being able to attend.
8. **Don't confuse opportunity with outcome:** log application/request and eventual outcome separately, including unknown/no-response states.

## Research conclusion

The evidence supports a cautious conclusion: talent identification depends on more than a player's self-description or a highlight video. It involves contextual performance evidence, human judgment, development pathways, and the opportunity to be observed. South African practitioners have also raised concerns about grassroots resources, regional coverage and pathway coordination.

For ScoutSA, the defensible direction is to **organize credible, contextual player evidence and genuine opportunity information without claiming to predict talent or guarantee access**. Whether this solves a meaningful Vaal Triangle problem remains unproven and should be checked against individual players' accounts in the next research phase.
