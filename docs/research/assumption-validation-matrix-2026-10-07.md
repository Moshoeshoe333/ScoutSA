# ScoutSA Assumption Validation Matrix — Primary Validation Phase

**Date:** 2026-10-07  
**Status:** RESEARCH CONTROL — NOT RATIFIED  
**Phase:** Primary Validation OPEN  
**Implementation:** HOLD

## Meta-question

> **What does ScoutSA uniquely need to solve that the existing football ecosystem does not already solve?**

Two honest terminal outcomes are defined in advance:

1. If evidence shows ScoutSA adds little beyond existing trusted structures, the product thesis must change or narrow.
2. If evidence shows a specific unresolved junction between trusted player evidence and legitimate opportunity access, that junction becomes the candidate differentiated thesis.

This matrix is an evidence-control instrument. It does not authorize Constitution, MVP, schema, RLS, migration, production, legal-compliance or safeguarding changes.

## Evidence lifecycle

**ASSUMPTION → DESK EVIDENCE → PRIMARY EVIDENCE → CONTRADICTION → INTERPRETATION → DECISION → RATIFICATION**

### Status vocabulary

- **OPEN** — insufficient primary evidence.
- **SUPPORTED** — evidence supports the assumption; not automatically ratified.
- **CHALLENGED** — meaningful evidence weakens or qualifies it.
- **FALSIFIED** — evidence materially contradicts the assumption.
- **RATIFIED** — separately approved through ScoutSA governance.
- **BOUNDED** — intentionally retained outside current scope.
- **RETIRED** — no longer used as a product assumption.

### Evidence rules

1. Direct stakeholder experience outranks our expectation about stakeholder behaviour.
2. A specific lived case is stronger than a general opinion.
3. Broker/introduction events are access evidence only; they are not stakeholder validation.
4. Public-source contact relevance is not stakeholder agreement.
5. A bounce/non-delivery is a routing fact, not rejection.
6. Contradictions are recorded, not discarded.
7. No implementation follows from this matrix without separate ratification.
8. A status change normally requires convergence across at least three materially different stakeholder roles, or one unambiguous falsification.
9. “No contradiction reported” is not equivalent to “confirmed.”
10. Legal or safeguarding conclusions require appropriately scoped external review; stakeholder opinion is not legal advice.

## Assumption register

### A1 — Talent exists widely, while genuine opportunity is comparatively scarce

**Current state:** OPEN  
**Desk evidence:** Strong support across Vaal/SA research, but local lived prevalence remains to be tested.  
**Primary evidence required:** Ask stakeholders for concrete examples of promising players who did and did not reach genuine opportunities.  
**Disconfirming evidence:** Stakeholders consistently report that opportunity is abundant and that lack of player quality is the dominant bottleneck.  
**Interpretation if challenged:** Reassess whether the problem is opportunity scarcity, player preparation, information quality, or another bottleneck.  
**Implementation:** HOLD.

### A2 — Visibility alone is not the core problem; trusted reachability may matter more

**Current state:** CHALLENGED  
**Desk evidence:** Research indicates relational trust, safety, access and invitations may matter more than simply making a profile visible.  
**Primary evidence required:** How did the last promising player come to attention? Who recommended them? What caused the receiving stakeholder to act?  
**Disconfirming evidence:** Stakeholders consistently say discoverability through an open digital system is sufficient and human trust adds little.  
**Interpretation if falsified:** Preserve/reconsider a visibility-led discovery model.  
**Implementation:** HOLD.

### A3 — Stakeholders need trustworthy evidence before progressing a player

**Current state:** OPEN  
**Desk evidence:** Strong hypothesis involving registration, trusted humans, event evidence and supporting media.  
**Primary evidence required:** What is checked first, second and third when a player is recommended? What causes rejection?  
**Disconfirming evidence:** Stakeholders routinely progress players without meaningful verification or rely primarily on self-report.  
**Interpretation if falsified:** Reduce verification burden and avoid unnecessary evidence layers.  
**Implementation:** HOLD.

### A4 — MySAFA / football registration may be a significant identity or verification anchor

**Current state:** OPEN  
**Desk evidence:** Strong secondary evidence supports registration/identity relevance, but Vaal behaviour is unverified.  
**Primary evidence required:** Is MySAFA actually checked? By whom? At what stage? What happens when registration conflicts with other evidence?  
**Disconfirming evidence:** Relevant stakeholders rarely use or trust registration information in actual decisions.  
**Interpretation if falsified:** Do not elevate MySAFA into a load-bearing ScoutSA field.  
**Implementation:** HOLD.

### A5 — Trusted human standing matters independently of a digital profile

**Current state:** OPEN  
**Desk evidence:** Word-of-mouth, coach, LFA and event relationships appear repeatedly in research.  
**Primary evidence required:** Who can vouch for a player, and why is that person's standing trusted?  
**Disconfirming evidence:** Stakeholders consistently treat all recommendations as equivalent regardless of recommender standing.  
**Interpretation if falsified:** Avoid building recommender authority into ScoutSA.  
**Implementation:** HOLD.

### A6 — Major-club opportunity pathways are often invitation/scouting-led rather than open-trial-led

**Current state:** CHALLENGED  
**Desk evidence:** Secondary research indicates invitation and showcase pathways may dominate for major clubs, while smaller academies may use open trials.  
**Primary evidence required:** How did the last recruited/referred player receive an assessment or invitation? How common are open trials?  
**Disconfirming evidence:** Vaal stakeholders report open trials as the dominant pathway even for major recruitment.  
**Interpretation if falsified:** Reconsider opportunity aggregation as a central product function.  
**Implementation:** HOLD.

### A7 — Existing Vaal football structures already solve meaningful parts of the pathway

**Current state:** CHALLENGED  
**Desk evidence:** Local LFA, school, tournament, academy, mentorship and informal networks are documented.  
**Primary evidence required:** Which structures actually solve discovery, verification, exposure, referral and access? Where do they stop?  
**Disconfirming evidence:** Stakeholders consistently report no effective existing pathway or network.  
**Interpretation if falsified:** A broader ScoutSA coordination role may be justified, but only from evidence.  
**Implementation:** HOLD.

### A8 — ScoutSA should interoperate with trusted existing systems rather than replace them

**Current state:** OPEN  
**Desk evidence:** Existing-system density supports this hypothesis.  
**Primary evidence required:** What systems do stakeholders already use? What information can realistically move between them? What duplication would be harmful?  
**Disconfirming evidence:** Stakeholders explicitly prefer a new independent system that replaces existing processes.  
**Interpretation if falsified:** Reassess integration versus replacement strategy.  
**Implementation:** HOLD.

### A9 — Guardian involvement is a meaningful safety and access mechanism for youth

**Current state:** OPEN  
**Desk evidence:** Strong safeguarding rationale and secondary evidence; practical workflow remains untested.  
**Primary evidence required:** How do guardians currently approve, reject, monitor or verify football opportunities? What causes concern?  
**Disconfirming evidence:** Youth pathways operate safely without guardian involvement in the relevant cases, or stakeholders identify another dominant mechanism.  
**Interpretation if falsified:** Narrow guardian controls rather than assuming universal centrality.  
**Implementation:** HOLD.

### A10 — Fraud/scam risk is material enough to affect opportunity trust

**Current state:** OPEN  
**Desk evidence:** Strong SA/global evidence around fake agents, trials and recruitment scams. Local prevalence remains unmeasured.  
**Primary evidence required:** What scams have stakeholders actually encountered? How are legitimate organisations/counterparties distinguished? Are payment requests a known warning sign?  
**Disconfirming evidence:** Multiple relevant stakeholders report negligible fraud exposure in their actual pathways.  
**Interpretation if falsified:** Reduce fraud-specific product scope while retaining ordinary trust controls.  
**Implementation:** HOLD.

### A11 — Practical access constraints materially affect whether an opportunity can be taken

**Current state:** OPEN  
**Desk evidence:** Geography, transport, safety and money are repeatedly indicated, but Vaal weighting is unknown.  
**Primary evidence required:** Which constraints actually caused recent players to miss opportunities? Which were decisive versus incidental?  
**Disconfirming evidence:** Stakeholders report that practical access rarely prevents otherwise suitable players from progressing.  
**Interpretation if falsified:** Reduce Opportunity Readiness constraint modeling.  
**Implementation:** HOLD.

### A12 — Education is not only a constraint; it can itself be an opportunity pathway

**Current state:** CHALLENGED  
**Desk evidence:** School, university, scholarship and education-first football pathways support this.  
**Primary evidence required:** Concrete examples of football-to-education opportunities and how they were discovered/accessed.  
**Disconfirming evidence:** Relevant stakeholders consistently describe academics only as a scheduling/eligibility constraint.  
**Interpretation if falsified:** Keep academic context limited to genuine constraints.  
**Implementation:** HOLD.

### A13 — ScoutSA has a specific unresolved junction worth solving

**Current state:** OPEN — DECISIVE  
**Desk evidence:** Candidate junction is between evidence-supported player information and legitimate opportunity access, but existing ecosystem overlap is substantial.  
**Primary evidence required:** At least three materially different stakeholder roles identify the same unresolved problem after naming who/what already solves adjacent parts.  
**Disconfirming evidence:** Stakeholders consistently identify existing structures as sufficient, or no common unresolved junction emerges.  
**Interpretation:** If falsified, narrow, redesign or retire the current product thesis. If supported, define the narrowest evidence-backed product boundary.  
**Implementation:** HOLD until ratification.

### A14 — Intended stakeholders will actually adopt or participate in a new ScoutSA system

**Current state:** OPEN  
**Desk evidence:** No meaningful primary adoption evidence yet.  
**Primary evidence required:** What would make a stakeholder use ScoutSA? What would stop them? What existing tool would they abandon, supplement or refuse to change?  
**Disconfirming evidence:** Stakeholders consistently say they would not use a new platform and existing workflows are sufficient.  
**Interpretation:** Product may need to become an interoperability/service layer rather than a standalone destination, or be retired.  
**Implementation:** HOLD.

### A15 — Opportunity economics and development/compensation incentives can affect pathway behaviour

**Current state:** OPEN  
**Desk evidence:** Secondary evidence indicates costs, training/development fees and compensation mechanisms may create incentives around player control and movement.  
**Primary evidence required:** What families/clubs actually pay; what clubs receive; what costs or disputes affect player movement; where incentives alter disclosure or access.  
**Disconfirming evidence:** Stakeholders report these economics have no meaningful effect on discovery, access or movement in the relevant Vaal pathways.  
**Interpretation:** Defer or remove economics modeling if not material to v1.  
**Implementation:** HOLD.

## Universal interview questions

Use these across stakeholder roles. Prefer concrete cases over abstract opinions.

1. **Discovery:** “Think of the last genuinely promising player you encountered. How did that player come to your attention?”
2. **Trust:** “What made you believe the information about that player was reliable?”
3. **Progression:** “What happened between first hearing about the player and the player getting an actual opportunity?”
4. **Failure:** “Where do promising players most often fall out of that process?”
5. **Existing solution:** “Who or what already helps solve that problem?”

### Required probes

Where relevant:

- What did you check first?
- Who made the introduction?
- Did you see the player live?
- Was registration checked?
- Was video useful?
- Was there an invitation or an open trial?
- What role did school/education play?
- What role did the guardian play?
- What practical barrier mattered?
- Did money change the decision?
- Have you encountered fake scouts/agents/trials?
- What existing system already handles this?
- What would make ScoutSA unnecessary?

## Disconfirming-evidence protocol

For every interview record:

**Claim/witness:** what the stakeholder actually said or described.

**Specific case:** the concrete player/pathway example, where available.

**Interpretation:** our analysis, kept separate from the witness.

**Contradiction:** evidence that conflicts with the current assumption.

**Disconfirming question:** what we asked to test the assumption.

**Result:** confirmed / challenged / contradicted / unresolved.

If no contradiction was offered, record **“none offered”** rather than treating absence as confirmation.

## Broker and contact evidence rule

A broker, journalist, coach, administrator or other intermediary may provide access to another stakeholder.

That access event is **not** itself evidence for the underlying assumption.

Therefore:

**Introduction → access event → stakeholder witness → evidence**

not:

**Introduction → validation claim**

Public-source identification establishes contact relevance only.

A delivery failure such as the Thomas Thema bounce is a routing/delivery fact and must not be interpreted as stakeholder rejection.

---

## Primary-validation decision rule

No material product assumption becomes ratified merely because desk research supports it.

A candidate product change becomes eligible for governance consideration only after:

1. relevant stakeholder evidence exists;
2. contradictions have been recorded;
3. existing ecosystem solutions have been identified;
4. the unresolved problem is specific enough to state;
5. the proposed change is no broader than the evidence requires.

**No schema, RLS, migration, production, legal-compliance or safeguarding claim follows automatically from this matrix.**
