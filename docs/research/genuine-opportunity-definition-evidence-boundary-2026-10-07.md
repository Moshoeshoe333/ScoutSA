# ScoutSA Genuine Opportunity Definition & Evidence Boundary

**Status:** RESEARCH / DESIGN INPUT — NOT RATIFIED  
**Date:** 2026-10-07  
**Authority:** None beyond the evidence and methodology already recorded in the repository  
**Governing principle:** Strength of claim must never exceed strength of evidence.

## 1. Purpose

ScoutSA's North Star is **verified players receiving genuine football opportunities**.

The phrase "genuine opportunity" is therefore important enough to require an explicit evidence boundary. This document proposes a working definition for research and external validation.

It does **not** establish a ratified product requirement, legal definition, safeguarding clearance standard, database schema, access policy, metric target, or implementation rule.

## 2. Working Definition

A **genuine football opportunity** is a real, relevant and sufficiently evidenced possibility for a player to participate in a defined football-related opportunity, where the opportunity has an identifiable responsible party and enough contextual information to distinguish a credible opportunity from mere visibility, communication, or an unsupported claim.

The definition should be evaluated across several dimensions rather than by a single badge or score.

## 3. Proposed Evidence Dimensions

### GOO-01 — Existence

There must be evidence that the opportunity actually exists.

Examples of stronger evidence:
- identifiable organization, team, academy, school, competition, trial or event;
- identifiable responsible party;
- defined opportunity record or attributable invitation;
- evidence tied to a time or relevant period.

A profile view, search result, advertisement, or unverified message does not by itself establish that a football opportunity exists.

### GOO-02 — Legitimacy / Responsible Party

The opportunity should have an identifiable party responsible for it, with authority or role relevant to the opportunity.

This does **not** mean:
- the person is automatically trustworthy;
- the organization is legally compliant;
- the opportunity is safe;
- the person has authority over every related action.

Identity/role verification and safeguarding clearance remain separate domains.

### GOO-03 — Relevance

The opportunity must be relevant to the player's stated or evidenced football context.

Relevance may depend on:
- age/category where legitimately applicable;
- gender/category where legitimately applicable;
- playing level;
- position or development context;
- geography;
- eligibility requirements;
- opportunity purpose.

Relevance is not a talent ranking and must not silently become one.

### GOO-04 — Practical Accessibility

The opportunity must contain enough information to evaluate whether the player can realistically participate.

Potential contextual factors include:
- date/time;
- location;
- transport/access constraints;
- required equipment or documentation;
- guardian involvement where applicable;
- stated eligibility or prerequisites.

A theoretically suitable opportunity is not necessarily accessible to a particular player.

Opportunity Readiness therefore remains contextual, not a universal score.

### GOO-05 — Authorization / Safeguarding Boundary

Where a player is a minor or protected context applies, contact, participation, disclosure and other actions must remain within the applicable authorization and safeguarding boundaries.

Important separations remain:

- discovery ≠ contact authorization;
- contact authorization ≠ safeguarding clearance;
- consent for one purpose ≠ blanket authorization;
- guardian authority ≠ universal authority;
- child voice ≠ guardian authority;
- historical authorization ≠ current authorization.

A claimed opportunity cannot be treated as genuine merely because someone recorded a contact or invitation.

### GOO-06 — Temporal Validity

An opportunity is time-sensitive.

Evidence should preserve enough temporal context to distinguish:
- an opportunity that existed historically;
- an opportunity that is currently open;
- an opportunity that has expired, changed, been withdrawn, or become unavailable.

Historical opportunity evidence must not silently become a current opportunity.

### GOO-07 — Evidence / Provenance

The basis for treating an opportunity as genuine must remain attributable and reconstructable.

The record should distinguish, where applicable:
- assertion;
- evidence;
- verification;
- derived state;
- decision.

No one of these objects becomes truth merely because it is stored.

## 4. What Does NOT Count by Itself

The following should not, by themselves, be treated as evidence of a genuine opportunity:

| Event / signal | Why insufficient alone |
|---|---|
| Player profile created | Establishes platform activity, not an opportunity |
| Profile view | Establishes visibility, not an offer or pathway |
| Search result | Establishes discovery, not legitimacy |
| Scout/coach interest signal | May be interest, but lacks sufficient opportunity context |
| WhatsApp/SMS/message received | Communication is not necessarily an opportunity |
| Contact request | Request is not approval or opportunity |
| Contact approval | Authorization to communicate is not proof of a football opportunity |
| Trial invitation | Potentially an opportunity, but still requires enough evidence of existence, relevance, responsible party and applicable safeguards |
| Opportunity listed by ScoutSA | Platform record is not proof that the real-world opportunity exists |
| Opportunity marked "verified" | Verification must remain scoped to defined claims; it cannot silently establish safety, suitability or guaranteed access |
| Trial attendance | Stronger real-world event evidence, but not proof of placement or success |
| Placement/selection claim | Must be evidenced separately; it is a downstream outcome, not automatic proof of every prior claim |

## 5. Proposed Minimum Threshold for a "Genuine Opportunity" Claim

For research purposes, a future ScoutSA claim that a player received a genuine opportunity should require evidence supporting at least:

1. **Existence** — the opportunity existed;
2. **Responsible party** — an identifiable relevant party was responsible for it;
3. **Relevance** — it was materially applicable to the player/context;
4. **Actionability** — there was a concrete pathway to participate, respond, trial, attend, or otherwise engage;
5. **Temporal context** — the opportunity was applicable during the relevant period;
6. **Authorization/safeguarding context** — any required protected-context boundaries were respected;
7. **Provenance** — the basis for the claim can be reconstructed.

This is a proposed evidence threshold, **not yet a ratified production rule**.

## 6. Stronger Evidence Tiers

A future evidence model may distinguish:

**Tier A — Platform signal**
- view, search, click, message, request.

Useful for activity analysis, but not sufficient for a genuine-opportunity claim.

**Tier B — Attributable opportunity evidence**
- attributable invitation;
- structured opportunity with responsible party;
- independently corroborated event information.

Potential evidence of opportunity existence, subject to relevance and context.

**Tier C — Confirmed real-world opportunity event**
- documented invitation/acceptance and attendance;
- attributable trial participation;
- other independently reconstructable participation event.

Stronger evidence that an opportunity became a real-world event.

**Tier D — Real-world outcome**
- evidenced placement, progression, registration, selection, or other defined downstream outcome.

This is stronger evidence of an outcome, but does not prove every upstream claim automatically.

The tiers are conceptual and must be tested against stakeholder practice before ratification.

## 7. Fraudulent, Impossible, or Misleading Opportunities

An opportunity should not count as genuine merely because it was recorded as one.

Examples requiring exclusion, contradiction, or unresolved status include:
- impersonated or non-existent organization;
- fabricated invitation;
- materially false event details;
- expired event presented as current;
- impossible location/time combination;
- opportunity outside stated eligibility without an identified exception;
- contact that lacks required authorization in a protected/minor context;
- evidence that has been withdrawn or contradicted such that current applicability cannot be established.

The correct response to uncertainty is not to invent certainty. Depending on evidence, the state should remain **unresolved, contradicted, expired, withdrawn, or not established** rather than being upgraded to genuine.

## 8. Opportunity ≠ Outcome

The following chain must remain explicit:

**Opportunity exists ≠ player received invitation ≠ player accepted ≠ player attended ≠ player was selected ≠ player was placed ≠ player progressed.**

Each transition is a separate claim requiring its own evidence.

This prevents the North Star from becoming a vanity funnel where an opportunity listing is counted as an impact outcome.

## 9. Relationship to Existing ScoutSA Boundaries

This proposal is consistent with, but does not amend, the existing methodology:

- Player Passport ≠ truth;
- Assertion ≠ Evidence;
- Evidence ≠ Verification;
- Verification ≠ Safeguarding Clearance;
- Football Readiness ≠ talent ranking;
- Opportunity Readiness ≠ selection guarantee;
- Discovery ≠ contact authorization;
- Historical authorization ≠ current applicability;
- platform event ≠ real-world event;
- opportunity ≠ outcome.

It also preserves the constitutional rule that legal compliance and safeguarding claims require evidence beyond documentation.

## 10. External Validation Questions

The next evidence source should be real football stakeholders.

Ask them, without leading:

1. What would make you personally regard a football opportunity as real?
2. What information do you need before trusting that an opportunity is genuine?
3. What makes an invitation credible or not credible in your current practice?
4. When does a message or WhatsApp contact become an actual opportunity, if at all?
5. What would make you reject or ignore an opportunity even if it appeared relevant?
6. What evidence do you keep after a player is invited to a trial?
7. How do guardian or safeguarding requirements affect whether a minor can actually participate?
8. Which parts of this proposed definition are unnecessary, missing, or unrealistic?
9. What existing informal process already handles this today?

The answers should be captured as stakeholder evidence, including contradictions. They must not be forced into the proposed model.

## 11. Open Questions

External evidence is still required for:
- what stakeholders consider minimally sufficient evidence;
- whether responsible-party verification is practical at MVP scale;
- how "relevance" should be operationalized without creating a talent-ranking system;
- how accessibility constraints should be represented without over-collecting sensitive data;
- what evidence can safely be retained for minors;
- how opportunity withdrawal/expiry is handled in practice;
- what real-world events can be independently evidenced;
- how informal WhatsApp and club networks should interact with a formal opportunity network;
- whether the proposed evidence tiers match actual stakeholder workflows.
