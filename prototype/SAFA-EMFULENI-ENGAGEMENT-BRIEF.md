# ScoutSA — Exploratory Engagement Brief: SAFA Emfuleni / ELFA

**Document status:** draft for discussion; not an agreement, endorsement or partnership claim  
**Proposed starting region:** Emfuleni / Vaal  
**Prepared:** 16 November 2026

## 1. Why we are reaching out

ScoutSA is being designed as a grassroots football discovery and development network. We want to start by understanding and supporting the football structures already operating in Emfuleni, rather than creating a parallel football authority.

Our intended first focus is to improve the visibility and organisation of the local football ecosystem: teams, age groups, competitions, tournaments, coaches, schools and development pathways. Over time, we want to help permissioned and properly reviewed player evidence reach appropriate football opportunities beyond the Vaal.

We recognise SAFA and its Local Football Association structures as the relevant football governance environment. ScoutSA does not claim to represent SAFA, ELFA, MySAFA or any club.

## 2. What ScoutSA would like to understand first

We would value guidance on:

1. The current ELFA structure, competition streams and localities.
2. Current-season affiliated clubs and teams, separated by age group, gender and competition.
3. How new, inactive, renamed, merged or unaffiliated community teams should be represented.
4. The current junior, senior, women's and girls' competition calendars.
5. Tournament organisers and existing development initiatives that should be included in the Vaal coverage map.
6. The correct process for confirming a club's affiliation and an authorised coach or team representative.
7. Existing player-development, scouting and academy referral pathways.
8. The rules and permissions required before independent observers, photographers or statisticians can work at an event.
9. Safeguarding, child participation, parent/guardian communication and escalation expectations.
10. Whether there is a low-risk way to demonstrate the concept using only synthetic or aggregate information.

## 4. Proposed value to explore

Subject to ELFA's guidance and approval, ScoutSA would like to explore whether it can help:

- maintain a dated, locality-based directory of teams and development structures;
- make competition and tournament pathways easier to understand;
- capture consistent match statistics and coach observations at approved events;
- create permissioned media references and event recaps with local creators;
- help authorised coaches and development staff review player evidence;
- route suitable, permissioned profiles to verified opportunities through a controlled human-review process.

These are proposed capabilities, not services already operating.

## 5. A low-risk first collaboration to discuss

We suggest beginning with a mapping and learning exercise, not a live player-data integration.

Possible first activity:
- compare ScoutSA's public-source research seed register with ELFA's current team/competition information;
- identify missing localities, teams, age groups, women's/girls' teams and competitions;
- map the next suitable tournament or development event;
- agree whether aggregate event information or synthetic demonstrations can be used to test the proposed workflow;
- document the correct permissions, responsibilities and boundaries before collecting any personal information.

No player records, MySAFA access, identity documents, guardian records, private contact details or footage are requested as part of this initial conversation.

## 6. Safeguarding and data boundaries

ScoutSA intends to work within the authority of the relevant football and child-safeguarding structures. Before any live youth-facing activity, the model must have appropriate legal and specialist review and clear operational controls.

Non-negotiable design intentions:
- no player pays to be seen;
- no public minor contact details;
- no direct private scout-to-minor contact;
- no photography, video or player-level data capture without the relevant permissions;
- no profile referral to an agency without recipient checks, purpose-specific player permission, valid guardian authority where required, and recorded human release approval;
- no claim that a statistic, profile or scouting recommendation guarantees selection, trial, contract or placement;
- no claim of POPIA, Children's Act, FIFA, SAFA or MySAFA compliance or integration without the necessary evidence and authorization.

## 7. Questions for the first meeting

1. What is the most reliable current source of ELFA team and competition membership?
2. Which localities and streams must be included to avoid overlooking teams?
3. Who is authorised to confirm affiliation and current-season participation?
4. What are the existing pathways for identifying and referring talented players?
5. What is already working well in tournament statistics, photography and coach development?
6. What would make ScoutSA useful rather than duplicative?
7. What must ScoutSA never do?
8. What would be a safe, measurable first demonstration?

## 8. What we are asking for

We are asking for an exploratory discussion and guidance on the appropriate process—not endorsement, data access, an MoU or a commitment to pilot.

If the concept is not useful to ELFA, we would rather learn that early and adapt it.

## 9. Proposed meeting outcome

A successful first conversation would produce:
- the correct structure and contact pathway;
- a list of authoritative sources to reconcile against;
- a clear boundary between public directory information and restricted personal information;
- a shortlist of suitable tournaments or school programmes to learn from;
- a decision on whether a synthetic or aggregate-data demonstration is worth reviewing.

**ScoutSA's position:** complement existing football structures, respect their authority, and make the grassroots pathway more visible without compromising player dignity, safety or control.
