# ScoutSA Opportunity Requirement Declaration — Proposal

**Status:** Research/design proposal — **NOT RATIFIED**  
**Branch:** `prototype/internal-demo`  
**As of:** 2026-11-13  
**Authority:** None. This document does not amend the Constitution, Domain Model, Relationship Model, schema boundary, review-engine contract, or implementation permissions.

## 1. Purpose

The [Opportunity Evidence Acceptance Matrix](OPPORTUNITY-EVIDENCE-ACCEPTANCE-MATRIX.md) asks what evidence can establish a particular opportunity requirement. This declaration turns that question into a proposed, reviewable record that an opportunity owner would complete before an opportunity could be considered for publication or player-facing discovery.

It is a requirements-disclosure template, not an approval form and not an implemented workflow. A completed declaration would still require independent review. Submission must never be treated as verification, publication, invitation, eligibility, safeguarding clearance or a promise of placement.

**Core rule:** an opportunity owner must describe the actual offer and each material requirement precisely enough that another reviewer can challenge its authority, evidence, fairness, safety, scope and expiry.

## 2. Opportunity identity and accountable ownership

The proposed declaration should record:

- A stable internal opportunity reference and version.
- The named organization or accountable individual making the offer.
- The legal/operating identity claimed by that owner, with the exact evidence source and verification status kept separate.
- The representative submitting the declaration, their claimed role, and evidence that they are authorized to act for this specific organization and opportunity.
- A reliable organizational contact route suitable for adult-to-adult operational communication; do not expose private contact details publicly.
- Any intermediary, agent, recruiter, sponsor or referral party, their relationship to the owner, and the scope of their authority.
- Conflicts of interest, financial interests, referral fees or other incentives relevant to the offer.

An organization identity check does not prove representative authority. A representative's identity does not prove authority to make this offer. A logo, social profile, email domain or self-description is a lead, not sufficient proof by itself.

The person or organization asserting authority must not be the sole reviewer of that same authority claim. Independent review and an auditable reason are proposed safeguards; the exact role model remains unresolved.

## 3. Describe what is actually offered

The owner must select and describe the real-world activity without using a vague label such as “opportunity” or “scouting exposure” as a substitute for detail.

Candidate offer types:

- Open community development session.
- Recurring community development programme.
- Academy assessment or trial.
- Club or academy membership.
- League or competition registration.
- Scouting observation or talent-identification event.
- Scholarship, bursary or funded development programme.
- Other — with a plain-language explanation.

For the specific offer, declare:

- What the participant will actually do and what the owner will provide.
- Whether the activity is training, assessment, observation, competition, registration, education, funding or another defined activity.
- Whether attendance is open, application-based, invitation-only or subject to selection.
- Whether participation is free, paid or conditionally funded; disclose every fee, deposit, travel cost, equipment cost, commission or other expected payment, who receives it, when it is due, and whether it is refundable.
- Whether any payment is claimed to be required to be seen, considered, selected or placed. A pay-to-be-seen proposition must be escalated for explicit policy review; it must not be normalized as an ordinary entry condition.
- What is not included, what is not guaranteed, and what happens after the activity.

“Scouting exposure”, “professional pathway”, “scholarship opportunity” or “trial” must not be used unless the described activity supports that exact representation. Attendance is not selection; observation is not an offer; an invitation is not placement.

## 4. Target cohort, place, time and capacity

Declare the intended cohort and practical conditions:

- Age range or other target cohort, with a reason for any restriction.
- Whether the opportunity accepts players with informal/community experience or requires a defined formal status.
- Location, venue, access arrangements and responsible on-site adult/organization.
- Dates, start/end times, recurrence, application deadline and time zone where relevant.
- Available capacity and how places are allocated if demand exceeds capacity.
- Language, disability/accessibility, equipment, school, transport or other practical conditions.
- Whether transport, accommodation, meals, insurance or medical support are provided, not provided or conditional.
- What changes or cancellation process applies.

A geographic label is not proof that transport is feasible. A venue name is not evidence that the venue is booked or safe. Capacity and dates must be current and attributable to the owner.

## 5. Requirements: mandatory criteria versus preferences

Every criterion must be listed separately, using a stable criterion reference and plain language. For each criterion declare:

1. Exact proposition to establish.
2. Whether it is mandatory, a preference, or contextual information only.
3. Why it is relevant to the actual activity.
4. The rule, policy or operational reason relied on, including issuer, version and effective period where applicable.
5. Who has authority to set and interpret it.
6. The evidence that may establish it and why that evidence is proportionate.
7. Who reviews the evidence and who makes the resulting decision.
8. What happens if evidence is missing, disputed, expired, inaccessible or contradictory.
9. Whether an alternative route or reasonable accommodation is available.
10. How the criterion can be challenged or corrected.

Do not use undefined requirements such as “verified player”, “registered player”, “high potential”, “trusted coach” or “good standing”. Translate them into specific, testable propositions.

Do not make formal club membership a universal prerequisite. It may be relevant where a named competition or activity genuinely requires it, but it must not be silently imposed on open community participation, expression of interest or a scouting observation.

Separate mandatory rules from preferences. A preference must not be presented as a rule or used as an undisclosed rejection reason.

## 6. Evidence declaration — claim by claim

For each criterion, list evidence at the level of the exact claim, not as a general certificate of trust.

Proposed evidence record fields:

- Criterion reference and proposition.
- Claimant and submitter, including their relationship to the player or organization.
- Source/issuer and the source's authority for this exact proposition.
- Evidence type and a protected reference to the source material; avoid placing sensitive documents in a public opportunity record.
- Date created, date checked, relevant period/season and expiry/review date.
- First-hand or second-hand basis; method of observation or record retrieval.
- Independent corroboration, including whether sources share a common origin.
- Known limitations, missing fields, conflicts and counter-claims.
- Reviewer identity/role, decision date, rationale and scope.
- Decision state: not reviewed, insufficient, not established, disputed, accepted for this claim, rejected for this claim, or needs investigation.

The [Opportunity Evidence Acceptance Matrix](OPPORTUNITY-EVIDENCE-ACCEPTANCE-MATRIX.md) remains the companion proposal for opportunity-specific evidence. It does not create a universal evidence ladder or numeric trust score.

A source can establish only what it is competent and authorized to establish. A player report may preserve the player's account. A coach may attest to first-hand activity within their actual knowledge. Formal registration requires an authoritative registration source where that exact requirement genuinely applies. An evidence decision for one criterion must not be copied to another.

## 7. Safeguarding, age, guardian and safe-contact declaration

The owner must declare, before any player-facing activity is considered:

- Who is responsible for safeguarding at the venue and during the activity.
- The supervision arrangements, including ratios and the responsible adult roles; the applicable standard and qualified reviewer remain to be determined.
- The safe venue, arrival/departure and transport arrangements, including who is responsible for handover.
- Whether photography, video, recording, public posting or onward sharing is proposed, and the separate purpose and permission required for each.
- How concerns, complaints, emergencies and incidents are reported and handled.
- How the owner will prevent direct private contact between adults acting for the opportunity and minors.
- The guardian involvement and consent route required for the relevant activity, communication and media purposes.
- How the child's views and participation are heard without treating the child's voice as a substitute for required adult authority or safeguarding.
- Any agent/intermediary involvement and how the claimed licensing/authority status will be checked where relevant.

Unknown age must not be treated as proof of adulthood. Until a reliable process is established, the conservative under-18 safeguards remain the prototype's stated design boundary. No private minor contact, real consent collection or real-player processing is authorized by this proposal.

This declaration is not a safeguarding certification. A submitted answer is not proof that the arrangements exist or work. Qualified South African legal and child-safeguarding review is required before any real-world use.

## 8. Application, invitation and outcome stages

The owner must state which stages actually exist and who controls each transition:

- Discoverable information.
- Expression of interest.
- Application or request for consideration.
- Eligibility review.
- Invitation to attend.
- Attendance.
- Assessment or observation.
- Selection decision.
- Formal registration or enrolment.
- Offer, scholarship or placement.
- Outcome, correction or dispute.

For every enabled stage, declare the decision-maker, required evidence, notice to the participant, expected response time and appeal/correction route. These are distinct events. A decision at one stage does not imply the next.

No public trial listing, invitation, contact, application submission, player disclosure or placement action is authorized by this document.

## 9. Privacy and data minimization

Declare what personal information is genuinely needed at each stage, the purpose for each item, who would receive it, how long it would be retained, how it could be corrected or deleted, and the process for handling a request or incident.

Do not request a player's full Passport, identity document, school record, health information, guardian contact details or media merely because it might be useful later. Collect no data until the lawful purpose, authority, access, retention, security and child-related safeguards have been reviewed and authorized.

The owner must explain what can be assessed without disclosing player identity or contact details. An opportunity listing must not publish sensitive requirements or personal evidence that belongs in a protected review process.

No POPIA compliance, integration or legal basis is asserted by this template.

## 10. Uncertainty, disputes, changes and expiry

The owner must declare:

- What is currently confirmed, what is self-reported, what is pending and what is unknown.
- How a player, guardian, organization or source can challenge a criterion or evidence decision.
- Who independently reviews disputes and how the original assertion and counter-assertion are preserved.
- What events trigger immediate suspension or re-review (for example owner/representative change, venue change, changed fees, changed age criteria, safeguarding concern or expired authority evidence).
- When the declaration and each evidence item expire or must be refreshed.
- How applicants are notified when material terms change, and whether they can withdraw without penalty.
- Who can pause or withdraw the opportunity and how affected participants are informed.

A change to a material requirement must create a new version and re-review; it must not silently rewrite the conditions under which an earlier decision was made. “Not established” is neutral: it is not proof that a person is false, unworthy, unsafe or not talented.

## 11. Proposed review and publication controls

Candidate independent review roles:

| Review | What it checks | Independence boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Owner/organization identity | The entity behind the offer | Not self-certified solely by the submitter |
| Representative authority | Authority to act for the entity and offer | Not inferred from title, badge or email alone |
| Offer accuracy | Activity, dates, venue, capacity, costs and promises | Evidence tied to the named owner/source |
| Requirement quality | Relevance, clarity, mandatory/preference distinction and fairness | Owner proposes; separate reviewer challenges |
| Evidence authority | Source competence, provenance, freshness and scope | Claim-specific, not a general trust badge |
| Safeguarding | Adult responsibility, venue, supervision, transport, contact and incident route | Separate from eligibility and marketing |
| Privacy/access | Purpose, minimization, recipients, retention and correction | Qualified review before real data use |
| Final decision | Whether a specific release stage is permitted | Authorized decision-maker, reason and timestamp |

The owner cannot approve their own identity/authority evidence, waive safeguarding requirements, or convert an unresolved claim into a verified one. The actual roles, separation-of-duty policy and publication authority remain open decisions.

## 12. Adversarial acceptance scenarios

A future validation exercise should test at least these cases:

1. A community coach submits an open, free session without a formal club registration record.
2. An academy lists “verified affiliation” as mandatory but cannot explain the rule or its relevance to an open assessment.
3. A representative submits a genuine organization logo but no evidence of authority to make this offer.
4. A legitimate organization has an expired representative mandate or a former contact person.
5. The owner describes a free trial, then asks players for a fee to be considered.
6. The venue/date is not confirmed, but marketing language implies the event is booked.
7. The opportunity owner changes the age range or fee after applications begin.
8. A coach's first-hand attendance attestation is incorrectly used as proof of league registration.
9. A formal registration record is genuine but from the previous season.
10. The activity is football-eligible but has no adequate adult supervision, safe transport or guardian route.
11. An intermediary privately messages a minor or requests identity documents/payment.
12. A player disputes an eligibility decision and the owner wants to overwrite the original claim.
13. A scholarship asks for financial or school information before explaining the purpose, access or retention.
14. Two reviewers rely on the same unverified source and call their conclusions independent.

For every case, reviewers must identify the proposition, source authority, consequence of uncertainty, decision owner, safeguarding stop, participant notice and dispute path.

## 13. Open decisions before ratification

- Which opportunity categories belong in the MVP, and which are out of scope?
- Which fields are mandatory for a declaration versus conditional on offer type?
- What evidence establishes an organization's identity and a representative's authority?
- Who may approve an opportunity, and how are conflicts controlled?
- What criteria may be mandatory, and what fairness/appeal standards apply?
- Which sources are authoritative for club, league, school, funding and agent claims?
- What safeguarding standard, supervision model and incident process apply?
- What guardian/child participation/consent rules apply to each stage and purpose?
- What privacy, lawful basis, access, retention and deletion decisions are required?
- What is the minimum viable review and dispute process?
- What evidence is needed to show that owners and reviewers can actually perform the workflow in Vaal grassroots settings?

These are not answered by a template. They require stakeholder evidence and, where relevant, qualified legal, privacy and child-safeguarding review.

## 14. Gate and implementation boundary

**No implementation is authorized.** This proposal does not change:

- the review engine, its verified-membership rule, fixtures or tests;
- the Constitution, Domain Model, Relationship Model or schema boundary;
- database, RLS, API, authentication, UI, storage or disclosure behavior;
- guardian, consent, safeguarding, age-assurance or contact policy;
- opportunity lifecycle or publication permissions;
- mainline gates, implementation holds or pilot authorization.

Before ratification, validate the declaration with grassroots coaches, players/guardians, academies, competition actors and relevant independent reviewers. Record disagreements and missing proof. Any legal, privacy, child-safeguarding or competition-rule question requires qualified review. Only explicit governance ratification may authorize reconciliation into governing documents and downstream design.

**Current decision:** retain all existing controls. Treat every field and review role in this document as a candidate for validation, not an approved requirement.

---

**Evidence principle:** The owner may describe an offer; only evidence and an authorized, independent decision can establish a claim. A declaration is not verification.
