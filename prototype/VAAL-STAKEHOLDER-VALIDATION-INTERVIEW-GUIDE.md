# Vaal Stakeholder Validation Interview Guide

**Branch:** `prototype/internal-demo`  
**State as at:** 2026-11-13  
**Status:** OPTIONAL RESEARCH INSTRUMENT — NOT RATIFIED  
**Execution status:** NOT CONDUCTED  
**Dependency:** NONE — external responses are not a prerequisite for continued internal product development.

## 1. Purpose and operating decision

This guide provides a neutral way to gather feedback if a stakeholder becomes available. It is intended to test assumptions behind the Opportunity Validation Scenario Pack, Opportunity Evidence Acceptance Matrix and Opportunity Requirement Declaration.

**ScoutSA development will continue without waiting for interviews.** Outreach has already been attempted; unanswered requests are not a development gate. This guide is a parked validation instrument, not a task that must be completed before design, synthetic prototyping or other authorized internal work proceeds.

If feedback arrives later, record it as evidence with its source, date, role, scope and limitations. It may prompt a design review; it does not automatically ratify a policy or override governing decisions.

## 2. What this guide can and cannot establish

It can help identify:
- whether a workflow is understandable to a particular respondent;
- practical constraints, vocabulary and missing steps;
- possible false-acceptance and false-rejection risks;
- points where the proposed process may be burdensome or unsafe;
- disagreements between roles or operating contexts.

It cannot, by itself, establish market demand, stakeholder consensus, legal compliance, safeguarding effectiveness, institutional authority, product-market fit or permission to process real player data. A small or self-selected group is not representative of all South African grassroots football.

## 3. Participation safeguards

Before any conversation:
- Participation is voluntary. Explain the purpose, expected duration and intended use of notes.
- A respondent may skip any question or stop at any time without consequence.
- Do not record audio, video or photographs unless separate, explicit permission is obtained; written notes are sufficient.
- Do not promise confidentiality beyond what can actually be protected. Avoid collecting identifying details.
- Do not request player names, ID numbers, phone numbers, addresses, private messages, health records, school records, account credentials or copies of actual documents.
- Use only the fictional scenarios in the scenario pack. Do not invite disclosure of identifiable allegations or sensitive cases.
- Do not privately recruit or interview a minor. Any future child-participation research requires a separately designed, age-appropriate, guardian-aware and independently reviewed process. This guide does not authorize it.
- If a respondent raises an immediate safeguarding concern, stop the product interview and follow an appropriate, independently established safeguarding referral process. Do not investigate the allegation through this exercise.

## 4. Suggested session structure

Suggested duration: 25–40 minutes, adjusted to the respondent's availability.

1. Explain purpose, voluntary participation and boundaries (3–5 min).
2. Understand the respondent's role and operating context without collecting identifying details (5 min).
3. Discuss one or two fictional scenarios relevant to that role (10–15 min).
4. Explore fairness, evidence, safety, cost and operational burden (5–10 min).
5. Summarize what was heard and invite correction (3–5 min).

Do not attempt to cover every scenario in one session. Prioritize relevance and respondent comfort.

## 5. Opening script

“Thank you for considering this conversation. We are developing ScoutSA, a proposed platform intended to help grassroots football participants discover and assess opportunities. We are testing our assumptions, not asking you to endorse the product. The examples are fictional. You do not need to share names, private records or sensitive cases. You may skip any question or stop at any time. We will record role-level feedback and limitations, not treat your comments as approval or as representative of everyone. Is it okay to continue with written notes?”

If the person does not agree, do not proceed.

## 6. Role-specific question sets

### A. Grassroots coach or community organizer

- How do players in your setting currently hear about training, development sessions and opportunities?
- What information do organizers genuinely need before allowing a player to express interest?
- Which useful activities happen outside formally registered clubs or leagues?
- What would be unfair about requiring formal club registration for every grassroots activity?
- What can you personally attest to from direct observation, and what would be outside your authority to confirm?
- Who should be able to confirm that you are currently authorized to represent an organization?
- What practical barriers affect attendance (cost, transport, venue certainty, timing, equipment or supervision)?
- What would make a digital process too burdensome for a community coach?

### B. Parent or guardian

- What would you need to understand before considering an invitation for a child?
- Which details about the organization, representative, activity, venue, supervision and transport would matter most?
- What would make a consent request understandable and specific?
- How should a system respond if guardians disagree or authority is unclear?
- What information should never be visible to an unknown coach, scout or organization?
- What would make you distrust an opportunity, even if it appeared to come from a known organization?
- What would a safe, non-pressured way to decline or withdraw look like?

### C. Adult player

- How do you currently learn about opportunities, and what makes information credible?
- What information would you want to see before deciding whether to express interest?
- What would make a profile or evidence request feel excessive?
- How should a player correct an inaccurate claim or challenge a decision?
- What would make an opportunity inaccessible even if the football requirements were met?
- What should remain private until a specific, justified step is reached?

### D. Academy or club representative

- What do you need to assess before inviting someone to an assessment or trial?
- Which criteria are mandatory, and which are preferences?
- What is the difference in your process between an enquiry, an application, an invitation, attendance, selection and registration?
- What evidence supports the exact claims you need to make?
- Who has authority to publish or change an opportunity on behalf of the organization?
- How should changed dates, fees, criteria or venues be communicated?
- What would cause your staff to reject or abandon a platform workflow?

### E. League or competition representative

- Which records or sources establish current registration and competition eligibility in your context?
- How do you handle records from a previous season?
- Which forms of grassroots participation exist outside formal league registration?
- Who may issue or confirm an eligibility decision?
- What wording would prevent a community coach's attendance confirmation from being mistaken for formal registration?
- What correction or dispute route exists when records are inaccurate?

### F. Safeguarding practitioner

- Which proposed workflow points create foreseeable risks for children or vulnerable participants?
- Where must the system stop rather than attempt to make a judgment?
- What safeguards should exist before any communication, invitation or disclosure?
- What would make a guardian workflow unsafe, coercive or confusing?
- What should happen when age, authority, consent or supervision is unknown or disputed?
- Which risks cannot be addressed by interface design alone?

This conversation is not a substitute for a formal safeguarding assessment.

### G. Privacy or legal reviewer

- Which proposed data fields appear unnecessary for the stated purpose?
- At what stage, if any, is each category of personal information justified?
- Which access, retention, correction, deletion, consent and accountability questions require formal legal analysis?
- What child-related requirements must be resolved before any real-data workflow?
- Which claims in the product documentation would be too broad without additional evidence?
- What independent review and records would be needed before a real pilot?

This guide does not ask the respondent to provide a blanket compliance sign-off.

## 7. Fictional scenario discussion

Select only relevant cases from `OPPORTUNITY-VALIDATION-SCENARIO-PACK.md`. Ask the same neutral sequence:

1. “What do you understand is happening here?”
2. “What, if anything, is established by the information shown?”
3. “What remains unknown?”
4. “Who would be in a position to confirm that specific point?”
5. “What would be a fair next step for the player?”
6. “What could go wrong if the system accepts this too quickly?”
7. “What could go wrong if the system rejects or blocks it too quickly?”
8. “What additional information would be proportionate to request, if any?”

Avoid asking “Would you approve this?” as the only question. Approval language can hide uncertainty and role boundaries.

## 8. Cross-cutting probes

Use only where relevant:
- **Evidence:** What exact proposition does this record support? Is it current, attributable and independently sourced?
- **Authority:** Does the source have authority for this exact claim, organization, person and time period?
- **Fairness:** Could this rule exclude informal but legitimate grassroots activity? Could it admit a misleading claim?
- **Fees:** Is any payment requested to be seen, considered or invited? What is the payment actually for, and who independently verifies it?
- **Safety:** Are age, guardian involvement, supervision, venue and transport sufficiently clear?
- **Privacy:** Is the information necessary now, or can it wait until a later justified stage?
- **Change control:** Who can change the opportunity terms, and how would a participant know?
- **Disputes:** Can a person correct a record or challenge a decision without retaliation?
- **Operational burden:** Who performs each step, how long does it take, and what happens when that person is unavailable?

## 9. Note-taking template

Use one record per session. Keep the respondent description broad enough to avoid identification.

| Field | Record |
|---|---|
| Session ID | Non-identifying code |
| Date | YYYY-MM-DD |
| Respondent role | Broad role only |
| Context | General setting; no organization/person name unless expressly necessary and authorized |
| Consent to notes | Yes / No |
| Scenarios discussed | Scenario IDs |
| Direct observations | What the respondent actually said or demonstrated |
| Interpretation | Analyst interpretation, clearly separated |
| Supporting detail | Exact short paraphrase or approved quotation |
| Contradictions | Views that differ from other evidence |
| Limitations | Experience scope, uncertainty, possible conflicts |
| Follow-up permission | Yes / No; no contact details in this record |

Do not convert an analyst's interpretation into a respondent quote. If quoting, obtain permission and remove identifying detail.

## 10. Evidence handling and synthesis

For every material finding, create a claim record with:
- Claim ID;
- exact proposition;
- respondent role and session ID;
- date and context;
- direct statement or observation;
- source limitations and possible conflicts;
- corroborating or contradictory evidence;
- status: supported in this context, disputed, not established, or out of scope;
- possible design implication;
- decision authority required, if any.

Keep individual views separate. Do not count repeated opinions as independent corroboration when respondents share the same source, organization or incentive. Do not use a vote or simple majority to resolve legal, safeguarding or authority questions.

Preserve minority views and contradictory evidence. A single credible safety concern may warrant investigation even if most respondents disagree; it does not automatically prove the concern is true.

## 11. Decision thresholds

Feedback may justify:
- **Clarification:** improve labels, explanations or workflow wording without changing policy.
- **Design revision:** adjust a synthetic prototype and document the reason.
- **Research follow-up:** identify an unresolved factual or operational question.
- **Governance escalation:** return a material semantic, authority, privacy or safeguarding issue to the relevant decision process.
- **No change:** record why feedback is out of scope, unsupported or not material.

No interview, respondent count or positive comment automatically authorizes database, RLS, API, authentication, real-data processing, disclosure, deployment or pilot activity. A separate explicit gate decision is required.

## 12. Development continues independently

The internal build path remains active. Work may continue on:
- synthetic-data UI and workflow refinement;
- accessibility and interaction testing;
- deterministic review-engine tests;
- non-production architecture and interface boundaries where already authorized;
- product demonstrations that clearly label fictional data and limitations;
- documentation of assumptions, risks and unresolved decisions.

The following remain outside this guide's authority: real player/minor data, public child profiles, live contact or invitation workflows, real consent collection, production authorization, database migration, RLS, API, identity processing, deployment and pilot activity. Their existing holds remain in force.

**External validation is a future evidence opportunity, not a prerequisite for every internal development step.** A demonstrable product may make later conversations more concrete, but it does not prove demand or replace independent review.

## 13. Review checklist

Before closing a session, confirm:
- [ ] Participation was voluntary and the purpose was explained.
- [ ] Only fictional scenarios were used.
- [ ] No unnecessary personal or sensitive information was collected.
- [ ] Direct statements were separated from analyst interpretation.
- [ ] Disagreements and limitations were preserved.
- [ ] No endorsement, consensus, compliance or validation claim was inferred.
- [ ] No implementation or release gate was treated as approved.
